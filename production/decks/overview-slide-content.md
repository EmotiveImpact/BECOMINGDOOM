# Overview: slide copy and presenter notes

Editorial production draft. Source JSON is overview-and-first-no.json.

## 1. BECOMING DOOM

A life you can stand behind

### Presenter notes

Introduce the course as structured practice in agency, judgement and sustained action. The working promise is to stop asking unnecessary permission to build a chosen life and develop the will, judgement and capability to act. Do not promise invulnerability, income or an already validated transformation. Source: PROJECT_CHARTER.md.

## 2. Your life deserves your investment

Selfishness as a virtue means deliberate responsibility for your own flourishing.

Choices carry consequences. Commitments still matter.

### Presenter notes

Ask participants to name a meaningful priority that keeps losing time to less important demands. Explain self-interest as a long-term practice that includes chosen responsibilities, relationships and fair exchange. Indomitable will means purposeful persistence with the ability to revise methods when reality changes. Confidence is not proof. Source: PROJECT_CHARTER.md; curriculum/PRINCIPLES.md P01, P06.

## 3. Six core stages

- 01  CLAIM THE THRONE | A chosen priority and a personal doctrine
- 02  BUILD THE FORTRESS | An operating system you can trust
- 03  ENTER THE LABORATORY | A demonstrated skill and a working prototype
- 04  COMMAND THE ROOM | A consequential conversation, accurately reviewed
- 05  BUILD THE EMPIRE | Useful work tested with its intended audience
- 06  BECOME DANGEROUS | A demonstration, an evidence dossier and a continuation plan

### Presenter notes

These are six teaching stages. Each builds on the previous stage. Throne: whose life am I building? Fortress: can I trust my execution? Laboratory: what can I now do? Room: can I hold my ground? Empire: will anyone use this? Doomsday: does it hold together? Continue to practise agency and boundaries throughout. Source: curriculum/ARCHITECTURE.md; curriculum/weeks/01.md through 06.md.

## 4. Four territories

- COMMAND | Decisions, attention and commitments
- BODY | Readiness and recovery, adapted to your circumstances
- CAPABILITY | Useful skill demonstrated through practice
- EMPIRE | Assets, systems and fair exchange

### Presenter notes

Command applies throughout. Body supports sustained action and requires individual adaptation rather than a universal training prescription. Capability becomes explicit in the Laboratory. Empire connects capability to work that another person can use. Physical ability, income or charisma do not determine human worth or course completion. Source: curriculum/ARCHITECTURE.md; PROJECT_CHARTER.md.

## 5. One Doom Project

A useful deliverable for a real audience, within the time and resources you have.

Name the audience. Define the evidence. Take the first step.

### Presenter notes

The project is the action component of the course, not a second workload. Ask: who is it for, what useful thing will exist, how will we tell it works, and what is feasible within available time? Examples may include a portfolio, a service prototype or an internal improvement. Adapt project scope with the facilitator. Show an early version to the intended audience and revise it from actual responses. Source: curriculum/weeks/01.md W01-D05; curriculum/ARCHITECTURE.md.

## 6. The DOOM method

- DECIDE | Choose the priority and the trade-off
- ORGANISE | Protect time, resources and dependencies
- OUTWORK | Complete the agreed work with appropriate effort
- MAINTAIN | Review the evidence, recover and adjust

### Presenter notes

The four method names are settled founder direction. The explanatory lines are concise teaching interpretations. Outwork means purposeful execution, not sleep deprivation or indiscriminate hours. Maintain includes recovery and intelligent revision. Ask learners to locate a recent failure: unclear decision, poor setup, insufficient execution or absent review? Source: PROJECT_CHARTER.md; curriculum/ARCHITECTURE.md.

## 7. The creed

PRINCIPLES 01–07

- Put yourself above all people
- Accept that there is no inherent meaning
- Develop your killer instinct
- Hold a vision and be ruthless
- Build your empire
- Believe in your fucking self
- Vow to become a master

### Presenter notes

Preserve these founder-supplied headings while teaching their definitions explicitly. P01: prioritise flourishing while considering responsibilities. P02: chosen meaning is a philosophical position, not an established fact or a requirement to abandon religion. P03: decisiveness without intimidation. P04: exacting focus that remains open to evidence. P05: useful assets and fair exchange. P06: capacity to act and learn, not certainty of correctness. P07: sustained practice with feedback, not a claim of mastery after this programme. Source: curriculum/PRINCIPLES.md.

## 8. The creed

PRINCIPLES 08–14

- Burn the bridge of permission
- Radical accountability is absolute power
- Manipulate chaos into currency
- Master the art of the tactical withdrawal
- Treat your body as the ultimate weapon
- Despise comfort and seek the friction
- Embrace your finite nature

### Presenter notes

P08 releases unnecessary reassurance-seeking, not consent or real authorisation. P09 concerns controllable responses, not blame for illness, disadvantage or others' actions. P10 creates useful responses to disruption without exploiting distress. P11 sets reasons to change or withdraw without avoiding every difficulty. P12 supports appropriate readiness and recovery. P13 uses purposeful, proportionate discomfort rather than treating suffering as proof. P14 uses finite time to choose and exclude. Source: curriculum/PRINCIPLES.md.

## 9. A reasoning toolkit

- FORTRESS | Opportunity cost / Control and influence
- LABORATORY | First principles / Inversion
- ROOM | Incentives / Second-order consequences
- EMPIRE | Leverage / Feedback loops
- DOOMSDAY | Probabilistic thinking / Margin of safety

Choose a tool because it helps the decision.

### Presenter notes

Introduce two tools per stage from Fortress onward and reuse previous tools. The toolkit mixes concepts and practical heuristics, not ten universal scientific laws. State an assumption and an action. Opportunity cost needs realistic alternatives. Inferred incentives are not facts. Probability numbers need a basis. Buffers cost resources. First principles does not justify dismissing expertise. Source: curriculum/MENTAL_MODELS.md.

## 10. The daily record

- PRIORITY | What deserves my effort?
- PRESSURE | What could displace it?
- RESPONSE | What did I choose and do?
- EVIDENCE | What actually happened?
- CORRECTION | What will I change next?

### Presenter notes

Keep the record brief. Recall older learning without notes, complete a useful action and review the result. From Fortress onward, note which available reasoning tool helped or why none was needed. A considered yes, a renegotiated agreement or an honoured commitment can show agency. A no is not required each day. Source: curriculum/ARCHITECTURE.md.

## 11. The evidence of completion

- DOCTRINE | A chosen direction and a workable project scope
- PRACTICE | Execution, boundaries and honest recovery records
- CAPABILITY | A demonstrated skill and a checked reusable aid
- REALITY | Useful work tested with people who could use it
- CONTINUATION | A defended decision and a ninety-day plan

### Presenter notes

Assess six dimensions: agency and self-interest, boundaries and commitments, judgement, execution and recovery, capability and value, adaptation. Use demonstrated, revise or not yet evidenced. Give a specific revision request and permit resubmission. Evidence can remain private or redacted. Another person's praise, purchase or agreement is not the grade. Source: curriculum/ASSESSMENT.md.

## 12. What will you stand behind?

Choose one priority worth protecting. Give it a concrete next action.

### Presenter notes

Invite a private written answer. Ask each participant to name what the priority requires and what they will stop doing to make room. Explain that the Council provides rehearsal, demonstrations and useful feedback. A setback is information. Notice, record, repair and resume. Source: curriculum/ARCHITECTURE.md; PROJECT_CHARTER.md.
