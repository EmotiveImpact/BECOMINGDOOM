# Lesson: slide copy and presenter notes

## 1. THE FIRST NO

The courage to be disliked

W01-L02

### Presenter notes

0:00–1:00. Think about the last time you said yes while hoping the other person would withdraw the request. Perhaps you were busy, or had finally found an hour for something important. You agreed anyway, then felt irritated with them for asking. Return to the moment before you agreed. What did you want to protect? What did you believe would happen if you protected it? Outcome: identify a chosen priority, distinguish an obligation from approval pressure, and make or rehearse a considered response.

## 2. What did your yes displace?

THE PROTECTED SESSION

Thursday evening Finish the portfolio

THE NEW REQUEST

Thursday evening A non-urgent favour

One evening. A decision about where it goes.

### Presenter notes

2:00–3:00 worked scene. You have set aside Thursday evening to finish a portfolio. A friend asks for non-urgent help during that time. You want to keep the evening, but hear yourself preparing to agree. Saying yes and resenting the friend hides the choice. Attacking the friend for asking shifts responsibility. Name the concrete cost: the portfolio remains unfinished. Ask learners what their own last reluctant yes displaced. Do not imply every request should receive a no.

## 3. Your life deserves your investment

You can choose where your effort goes. You are responsible for the choice.

### Presenter notes

1:00–2:00 principle. We call this selfishness as a virtue: deliberate responsibility for your own flourishing. Freely chosen commitments and genuine responsibilities need attention. Other demands get accepted because we fear what refusing might make someone think. Learn to distinguish those cases. A request opens a decision. Courage to be disliked means allowing the possibility that a considered decision may disappoint someone.

## 4. The decision before the answer

- COMMITMENT | What have I already agreed to do?
- REQUEST | What am I being asked to choose now?
- APPROVAL PRESSURE | What am I afraid they will think?

An existing commitment needs honouring or renegotiation.

### Presenter notes

3:30–4:30 selection. Invite an ordinary situation with manageable consequences. Ask: have I already made a commitment? What would agreeing displace? If I were not trying to manage their opinion, what would I choose? There may be a good reason to agree. Make the reason explicit. A legitimate responsibility is different from approval pressure. If an existing promise cannot be kept, address it and renegotiate rather than pretending it never existed. Allow a sixty-second pause.

## 5. A clear response

“I can’t help on Thursday. I’ve set that evening aside.”

If pressed: “I’m keeping that time. Thursday won’t work for me.”

### Presenter notes

3:00–3:30 model, then rehearsal. Deliver the response calmly. It states availability without asking the friend to agree that the project matters. Offer another time only if genuinely willing and available. No invented excuse is necessary. If the friend asks whether the session can move, repeat the limit. The response does not guarantee the friend will be pleased. Use the course example as a model, not a compulsory script.

## 6. The rehearsal

- CHOOSE | One real situation with manageable consequences
- WRITE | One or two sentences that state your answer
- REHEARSE | A reasonable follow-up and your response

A considered yes, no or renegotiation can all show agency.

### Presenter notes

4:30–6:00. Read your response once. Remove invented excuses. Remove accusations that the person was wrong to ask. Check existing commitments. Imagine a reasonable follow-up and write what you would say. Pause for rehearsal. Guilt may appear. Record it as an experience, then examine whether you overlooked an actual responsibility. The feeling deserves attention but does not decide the issue. Use W01-D03 rather than adding a second assignment. Provide private rehearsal instead of mandatory public disclosure.

## 7. The field record

- PREDICTION | What did I expect would happen?
- ACTUAL RESPONSE | What happened, or what did rehearsal reveal?
- NEXT ACTION | What did I do with the choice I made?

Keep the record private where needed. A written account is enough.

### Presenter notes

6:00–7:00. Use the response in one appropriate situation. If none exists, rehearse with a partner or draft a response to a recent situation. Never manufacture conflict. Evidence is an honest account of a considered decision and response. Do not grade another person's approval. Check clarity, existing commitments, absence of invented excuses or accusations, and whether the learner actually invests in the protected priority. Assessment: demonstrated, revise or not yet evidenced. Do not require recordings or screenshots of others.

## 8. What will you do with the time you protect?

Tomorrow, recall the difference between an obligation and approval pressure.

### Presenter notes

7:00–8:00. A clear boundary can make room for a skill, a piece of work or a life repeatedly postponed. What you protect must receive your effort. Ask for a specific next action for the named priority. Tomorrow, recall the distinction between an obligation and approval pressure without reopening the notes. Accept a deliberate yes supported by sound reasoning. Do not reward hostility or push participants into serious risk. This is educational practice, not treatment.
