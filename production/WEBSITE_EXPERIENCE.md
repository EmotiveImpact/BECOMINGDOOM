# Cinematic website experience

Founder direction captured 22 October 2026. This is a production specification, not an implemented website.

## The experience

One coherent dark architectural world. A man in a hoodie sits on a stone slab or seat in a beautiful, restrained home. The same person and environment persist through three views. Visitors scroll through the argument for Becoming Doom while the background moves between those states. Short narrated passages and synchronised subtitles accompany deliberate checkpoints. The final section reveals the six-week invitation and countdown.

## Three visual states

1. Seated: a wide rear or three-quarter view, hooded man on stone seating, dark premium home, architectural light and quiet presence. First establish a person, not a costume.
2. The pillars: view towards a columned architectural space, keeping the same man, clothing, materials, light direction and believable geography. Exact camera orientation remains an art-direction decision until originals are found.
3. Facing us: frontal view revealing an ordinary man in a hood. No mask or sudden change of identity. The invitation is that the viewer can develop these capacities.

Create three approved still keyframes before video. For each adjacent pair, create a short controlled transition plus a quiet held state. Match lens feel, figure proportions, room layout, lighting, garment and colour grade. Produce landscape and deliberately composed mobile crops; do not assume a landscape centre crop will preserve the figure and text space.

## Narrative checkpoints: proposed content roles

A. The borrowed life: show the cost of approval-driven choices.
B. Self-interest: establish that the viewer's life deserves deliberate investment.
C. The decision: connect that choice with boundaries, courage and a mission.
D. The work: explain the cumulative six weeks and real-world project.
E. The proof: capability, judgement, action and Doomsday demonstration.
F. The invitation: actual dates, delivery, price when decided, countdown and enrolment.

Six narrative checkpoints can sit within three visual states. The mapping and words need a timed storyboard before production. Keep each audio passage around 10–20 seconds initially, then test actual pacing. Let people read at their own pace.

## Playback and scrolling

- Start with a visible sound choice. Muted video may begin where supported; audible narration should begin after the visitor enables it. Provide a complete silent reading path.
- Use scroll to select a chapter. Play its short passage once after it settles into view; do not restart audio on every scroll movement.
- Stop or gently fade the previous passage before starting the next. Never stack competing voices.
- If the visitor moves on quickly, advance rather than force them to finish an old passage.
- Provide replay, mute and pause controls. Returning to an earlier chapter should make replay available rather than unexpectedly replaying everything.
- Synchronise subtitles to the actual narration. Also retain readable chapter text so the proposition is available without audio.
- For the first prototype, use chapter transitions and held shots rather than assuming frame-by-frame video scrubbing will perform well on phones. Test before deciding the final mechanism.
- Honour reduced-motion preferences with static keyframes and simple transitions. Keep navigation and enrolment usable without media.
- Pause background media when offscreen. Supply poster frames and compressed video; test on an ordinary phone and slow connection.
