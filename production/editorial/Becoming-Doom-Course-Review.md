## The ruthless edit

Course review and production decisions

## The strongest part

The course has a real instructional spine: a protected priority becomes an operating system, a capability, a consequential conversation and work exposed to an audience. One project makes the ideas testable. Preserve that spine. The distinctive voice and monumental visual world can make the programme memorable without replacing the practice.

## The central risk

The creed is much more forceful than its ordinary examples. Without a bridge, the brand promises an extreme transformation while the teaching feels like a polite productivity course. The answer is greater specificity and higher standards of evidence: a difficult but proportionate decision, a visible trade-off, a result and a revision. Louder claims alone add no substance.

## What needs cutting

Cut repeated introductory explanations after learners have demonstrated them. Cut extra tasks that duplicate the Doom Project. Cut any passage that implies intensity, suffering or a dramatic identity declaration proves capability. Keep the original fourteen headings, but put the operational meaning beside every first teaching encounter.

## What needs strengthening

Use one running project across the six stages and show its revisions. Include a mistaken response, the cost of that response and a better attempt in every core lesson. Finish each teaching segment with a field action and an observable standard. Give each participant specific corrective feedback, then require a revision.

## A harder teaching standard

What changes in the next edition

## 1. A visible consequence

At the Throne, show the portfolio still unfinished because of unpaid revisions. At the Fortress, show the session lost to an unprotected environment. At the Laboratory, show an output failing a simple check. At the Room, show a vague request producing no decision. At Empire, show an offer nobody uses. At Doomsday, show the original plan meeting a changed constraint.

## 2. An explicit threshold

The evidence should answer: what did you do, how do you know, and what did you change? Keep the three-level assessment: demonstrated, revise or not yet evidenced. Completion should not depend on public confidence, income, a perfect streak or another person’s agreement.

## 3. A coherent role for the creed

Teach the philosophy as a chosen stance with precise boundaries. Self-interest includes long-term consequences and freely chosen commitments. Ruthlessness concerns focus. Killer instinct concerns timely decisions. Tactical withdrawal needs exit criteria. These definitions make the language usable rather than merely provocative.

## 4. A controlled increase in independence

Begin with a model and guided rehearsal. Move to a checklist, then a choice of tools, then independent judgement. Later work should require recall of earlier lessons. Do not introduce more tools when learners cannot yet use the existing ones.

## 5. A sharper Council

Use the Council for demonstrations and correction. Keep storytelling brief. Ask for the work, the decision, the uncertainty and the revision. Allow private evidence and redact unnecessary personal details. The founder should bring comparable evidence of their own work.

## Decisions before launch

Unresolved choices and a useful pilot

## Duration and naming

The recovered charter specifies six weeks and 42 daily missions. The original poster shows 61 days. The latest image series establishes artwork, not a verified ten-slide curriculum. This edition uses six teaching stages without a calendar promise. Decide whether 61 days includes preparation and consolidation, or whether the teaching duration changes. Then update the calendar, workbook IDs, promises and site together.

## Audience and commercial offer

The ambitious-adult audience remains a hypothesis. Price, launch date, cohort size, platform and facilitator availability are open. Interview prospective participants about their real stalled projects, willingness to practise and available time. Do not publish a precise transformation claim before evidence exists.

## Pilot design

The existing proposal is 6–8 adults, with baseline and final evidence, actual workload, withdrawal interviews and a 30-day follow-up. Keep those measures. Review a sample with two facilitators to calibrate judgements. Obtain separate consent for testimonials. A small pilot without a comparison group cannot establish that the course caused every improvement.

## Release gates

Before a paid launch: reconcile duration; complete and rehearse every core teaching segment; check all exercise instructions against the daily map; test accessibility and workload; establish private submission and feedback; agree a revision policy; verify source rights and claims.
