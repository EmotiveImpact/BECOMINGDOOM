# W01-L03: Become nothing; choose something

Video 3 of 18 • Week 1: Claim the Throne • Full lesson, version 2

- **Principles:** P02 Accept that there is no inherent meaning; P06 Believe in your fucking self
- **Model:** none new

- **Evidence:** The old label, the test action and the observed result

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** BECOME NOTHING. CHOOSE SOMETHING.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

You have a doctrine and you have said your first no. Now we deal with the voice that says you are not the kind of person who does this.

Lesson three: become nothing, choose something. We are going to take one label you carry about yourself and put it on trial. Tomorrow you choose the project that carries you to Doomsday, and this label is very often the thing that would have stopped you choosing it.

## Slide 2 | scene

**On screen:** I AM NOT THE KIND OF PERSON WHO...
**Visual:** scene: a man looking at himself in a bathroom mirror early in the morning, light from a small window, expression flat

**Say:**

Finish this sentence honestly. I am not the kind of person who...

Who sells. Who speaks in public. Who finishes things. Who is good with money. Who writes. Who leads.

Most people can finish that sentence in under a second. That speed tells you how often they have said it.

## Slide 3 | question

**On screen:** Is it a description, or a verdict?

**Say:**

That sentence sounds like a description. It sounds modest, even realistic.

In practice it works like a verdict. It decides what you will try before you have tried it. And because you never try, the verdict is never challenged, so it gets a little more convincing every year.

## Slide 4 | scene

**On screen:** ONE LIFE. MANY LABELS.
**Visual:** scene: old school reports, a sports-day ribbon and a faded photograph spread across a table under a lamp

**Say:**

Look at where labels come from. Not a business person. Bad with numbers. Not creative. Too old to start. Not a natural leader.

Each one began as a summary of a few moments. Often moments from years ago, sometimes from childhood, sometimes from a single comment by someone who barely remembers saying it. Then the summary hardened.

## Slide 5 | red

**On screen:** NO LABEL WAS WRITTEN INTO YOU.

**Say:**

Principle two says: accept that there is no inherent meaning.

This is a philosophical position, and you are free to hold it alongside a faith or any other belief you already have. You do not have to convert to anything. Applied here, it means something practical. No label was written into you. Nobody stamped bad with money on you at birth. Labels were made, which means you can hold one up and inspect it.

Becoming nothing means releasing the label. Your history stays. Your dignity stays. Your responsibilities stay. What goes is the verdict.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** AN EVENT. A STORY. A RULE.
**Visual:** scene: a teenage boy at the front of a classroom, paper shaking in his hand, faces turned towards him

**Say:**

Labels tend to form in three steps.

First, an event. One bad presentation. One failed attempt. One comment from a teacher, a parent or a manager.

Second, a story. You decide what that event means about you. Usually on the spot, usually harshly, and usually with no other evidence.

Third, a rule. The story hardens into a rule you follow for years without checking it again. I don't do presentations. I'm not a numbers person.

## Slide 7 | list

**On screen:** TURN THE VERDICT INTO AN EXPERIMENT.

**Say:**

So here is the move. You go back to step one and collect a new event. You turn the verdict into an experiment.

A verdict says what you are. An experiment asks what happens when you do one small thing. A verdict cannot be tested. An experiment can, and it produces evidence by the end of the week.

You do not need to believe the label is false before you start. You only need to be curious enough to check.

## Slide 8 | scene

**On screen:** I AM NOT A BUSINESS PERSON.
**Visual:** scene: Ama at a small desk with spreadsheets on two screens, a mug of tea, a sticky note on the monitor reading "one conversation"

**Say:**

Meet Ama. She is a skilled bookkeeper. She works for a firm, she is good at it, and she wants her own clients.

Every time she thinks about it, the same sentence arrives. I am not a business person. Business people are confident, they network, they enjoy selling. She is none of those things, so the idea goes back in the drawer.

## Slide 9 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to handle the label

**Say:**

There are three ways she could handle that label.

Obey it. She does nothing. The label is confirmed by default. Notice that it is confirmed by absence, with no evidence at all.

Hype over it. She tells herself she is a CEO now. She buys a domain, makes a logo and posts about her journey. It feels strong for an evening. It produces nothing anyone could check, and the first quiet week brings the label straight back.

Test it. She has one real conversation and writes down what happened.

## Slide 10 | scene

**On screen:** TWENTY MINUTES. NO PITCH. JUST QUESTIONS.
**Visual:** scene: two women at a café table, one with a notebook asking questions, the other, a shop owner in an apron, explaining with her hands

**Say:**

So she runs the experiment. She asks one small business owner, a woman who runs a florist near her flat, for twenty minutes to talk about the problems she has with her books. No pitch. Just questions.

She learns that the owner hates her current system, loses two evenings a month to it, and would happily pay someone to make the problem disappear.

That is information. The label has nothing to say about it. Ama still does not feel like a business person. She now has evidence that someone wants what she can do, which is far more useful than a feeling.

## Slide 11 | red

**On screen:** BELIEVE IN YOUR FUCKING SELF.

**Say:**

Principle six says: believe in your fucking self. It is the loudest line in the creed, so let us be precise about it.

In this course, belief is a decision to act. It is trust in your capacity to take the next step and learn from it. It has nothing to do with feeling certain, and it has nothing to do with being right. Confidence and correctness are different things, and confusing them gets people hurt.

You believe in yourself enough to try, and then you let the result teach you. That is the whole move.

## Slide 12 | scene

**On screen:** NINE YEARS. NO MICROPHONE.
**Visual:** scene: a warehouse at the start of a shift, a supervisor in a hi-vis vest holding a clipboard in front of a small team

**Say:**

A second case. Lewis supervises a warehouse team. Nine years ago he froze during a best man's speech, and he has avoided every microphone since. He tells people, I'm not a talker.

He lets the manager deliver every briefing, even the ones about his own team's work. It has cost him a promotion at least once.

## Slide 13 | story

**On screen:** Two minutes. Notes allowed.

**Say:**

His experiment is two minutes at Monday's briefing, explaining one safety change. Notes allowed. Nobody needs to be impressed.

His voice shakes for about twenty seconds. Then it settles. Two people ask questions, which means they were listening. Afterwards the manager says, you should do that more often.

Two minutes proves nothing about whether Lewis is a great speaker. It proves something smaller and more important. The rule he had followed for nine years was wrong about what would happen.

## Slide 14 | scene

**On screen:** SMALL ENOUGH TO DO. REAL ENOUGH TO COUNT.
**Visual:** scene: a single sent message on a phone screen, "Could I ask you a few questions about your business?", with a reply typing

**Say:**

Keep your experiment small. One conversation. One message. Twenty minutes. Two minutes at a briefing.

Small enough that you will actually do it this week. Real enough that the answer counts. If the experiment needs a website, a business plan or a new wardrobe, it is too big.

## Slide 15 | list

**On screen:** EXPERIMENTS THAT PROVE NOTHING.

**Say:**

Here is where people go wrong.

Too big. You try to launch a business to test a label. If it wobbles, the label claims victory and you are worse off than before.

No record. You do the action but never write down what happened, so memory quietly rewrites it to fit the old story.

Moving goals. You decide what counts as success after you see the result. Decide first, then look.

And the rigged test. You pick an experiment that the label is certain to win, like cold-calling fifty strangers when you have never made one call. Fear is very good at designing tests it knows you will fail. Choose one you could plausibly pass.

## Slide 16 | scene

**On screen:** A SUMMARY. NEVER A LAW.
**Visual:** scene: a man crossing out a line in a notebook with one firm stroke, desk lamp, late evening

**Say:**

Three things to keep.

A label is a summary of a few old moments. It is never a law. Test it with one small action you could actually pass. And write down what happened before your memory edits it.

## Slide 17 | steps

**On screen:** PUT ONE LABEL ON TRIAL.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Write the label exactly as you say it to yourself. Use your own words, including the ugly ones.

Two. Name one small action that the label says you cannot do.

Three. Shrink that action until it fits into this week. If it needs a website, it is too big. If it needs one message or one conversation, it is about right.

Four. Decide in advance what result would count as evidence. A reply. A question asked. A conversation that lasts ten minutes. Write it down.

Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 18 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission: W01-D04
**Visual:** scene: a hand placing a phone face up on a table, a name ready in the contacts, a clock reading 7.58

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W01-D04, release the label.

Name one label. Take one small action that tests it. Record what actually happened, using the evidence line you wrote before you started.

Confidence is optional. Evidence is the requirement.

If your experiment involves another person, be honest about why you are asking. A research conversation stays a research conversation. Do not turn it into a sneaky sales pitch.

## Slide 19 | close

**On screen:** YOU ARE A WORK IN PROGRESS.

**Say:**

Tomorrow you choose the Doom Project: the one thing you will build over the next five weeks. Bring today's evidence with you. The project you choose should be one your old label would have vetoed.

You are a work in progress, and the work starts now. Choose something, then test it.

## Production notes

- Delivery: warm and steady. Play the hype option straight; never mock the people who try it.
- P02 is presented as a philosophical position participants may weigh. Make no claim that it is proven, and never ask anyone to give up a faith.
- P06: keep confidence and correctness separate on screen and in delivery.
- Ethics: the interview is a conversation, not a sales call. Participants must not misrepresent why they are asking.
- No outcome claims. Ama's and Lewis's results describe their cases only.
