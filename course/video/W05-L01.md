## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** FIND USEFUL LEVERAGE.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Welcome to week five: build the empire. You have a project, a system, a capability and the ability to hold your ground with people. Now we ask whether your work produces real value for someone else.

We start with leverage.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** Your hours are limited. WHERE DO THEY MULTIPLY?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

You only have so many hours, so much money and so much attention. That will not change much in the next two weeks.

So the question is where one of those scarce resources can produce far more useful output than it does now.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHERE CAN A SCARCE RESOURCE PRODUCE MORE USEFUL OUTPUT?

**Say:**

The seventh thinking tool is leverage. The question: where can a scarce resource produce more useful output?

Note the word useful. Leverage is measured by the result your audience gets, not by how busy you look.

## Slide 4 | list

**On screen:** WHERE THE MULTIPLIER LIVES.

**Say:**

Leverage comes in four common forms.

Clarity. Write something once so you never have to explain it again.

Systems. A template, a checklist or a process other people can follow.

Tools. Software that repeats a clear task for you.

People. Help, paid or swapped, on the parts others do better. Notice the order. Clarity usually comes first, because every other kind multiplies whatever it is given.

## Slide 5 | question

**On screen:** What problem does your audience actually have?

**Say:**

Before you multiply anything, be sure you know the problem your audience actually has. Not the one you assume. The one they describe when you ask.

Leverage applied to the wrong problem just produces the wrong thing faster.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** SAME QUESTIONS. EVERY WEEK. ONE CLEAR CHECKLIST.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Priya runs operations for a small team. Every new starter asks her the same fifteen questions in their first week, and each one takes her about five hours.

Her Doom Project is to fix this. Her first idea is to build an automated onboarding bot.

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to spend her leverage

**Say:**

Three ways to spend her leverage.

Automate it straight away. But the current instructions are unclear, so the bot repeats confusing answers to more people, faster. The limit on this tool: automating bad work scales bad work. Count quality and maintenance costs.

Keep answering by hand. Five hours per starter, forever.

Clarify first. She writes one clear onboarding checklist and tests it with the next starter. The questions drop. Then, if it still makes sense, she automates the parts that are stable.

Principle ten says manipulate chaos into currency. The chaos here is fifteen repeated questions. The currency is a checklist that saves hours for everyone who comes after.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Jamal is a personal trainer. Every client texts him the same food questions, often late at night.

His lever is a two-page food guide and three short videos, made once.

Within a month the texts drop by half. He measured it rather than guessed, and now he can decide what to build next.

## Slide 9 | stats

**On screen:** Write it as a testable claim.

**Say:**

Write your leverage idea as a hypothesis you can test. Today it costs this much. I will change this one thing. I expect this result, and I will know by this date.

Leave the result as a question mark until reality fills it in.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** FAKE LEVERAGE.

**Say:**

Three kinds of fake leverage.

Busy tools. Software that looks impressive and saves nobody any time.

Scaling mess. Multiplying a process before it works.

Forgotten upkeep. Ignoring the hours it takes to maintain the lever. Count them before you build.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Leverage is measured by the result your audience gets. Fix it before you scale it. And clarity is usually the first lever.
