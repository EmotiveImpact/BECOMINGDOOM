# W01-L01: Selfishness as a virtue

Video 1 of 18 • Week 1: Claim the Throne • Demo edition for founder sign-off

- **Runtime:** about 9 minutes (around 1,300 spoken words at a steady pace, plus two pauses)
- **Principles introduced:** P01 Put yourself above all people; P04 Hold a vision and be ruthless
- **Leads to:** workbook page W01-D02, Doctrine v1
- **Evidence:** a one-page doctrine that commits a specific resource, names what it displaces and addresses a real responsibility
- **Sources in play:** R03 (Rand on self-interest, as a philosophical position, not proof); the founder's creed
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block below is one slide. **On screen** is the slide copy. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

## Slide 1 | title | 0:00

**On screen:** Selfishness as a virtue
**Kicker:** Becoming Doom • Week 1 • Claim the Throne
**Visual:** title-seated

**Say:**

Welcome to Becoming Doom. This is the first lesson, and it starts with the idea the whole course stands on.

Selfishness as a virtue.

If that phrase makes you a little uncomfortable, good. Stay with it for nine minutes. By the end you will know exactly what we mean by it, what we do not mean by it, and you will have done something about it.

## Slide 2 | hook | 0:30

**On screen:** You keep saying this work matters. What has it actually been allowed to **cost**?

**Say:**

Think of the thing you keep saying matters. The business. The book. The body. The skill. The move.

Now answer honestly. In the last month, what has it actually been allowed to cost? Which evening did it win? Which request did it beat? Which comfortable habit did it replace?

If the honest answer is "nothing", then right now it is a preference. A wish with good manners. It only becomes a priority when it is allowed to take something that something else wanted.

## Slide 3 | image-right | 1:20

**On screen:** Doom does not wait to be **chosen**.
**Visual:** throne

**Say:**

Our emblem is Doom. In the stories, he is a man who decided what he would become and then built the capability to become it. He did not wait for a vote. He did not ask whether his ambition was acceptable to the room.

We take one thing from that and only one thing to start: authorship. He treats his own life as something to be built on purpose.

We also watch where he goes wrong. He confuses his judgement with a right to rule other people. We are not doing that. Your ambition gives you a claim on your own resources. It gives you no claim on anyone else's.

## Slide 4 | statement | 2:00

**On screen:** Your life is a legitimate object of your own **ambition**.

**Say:**

So here is the proposition.

Your life is a legitimate object of your own ambition. Your future has a real claim on today's time, attention, money and effort. You do not need to make your ambition socially unobjectionable before you are allowed to pursue it.

That is what we mean by selfishness as a virtue. Deliberate responsibility for your own flourishing. You choose what you are building, you protect what it needs, and you own the consequences of that choice.

Principle one in our creed says: put yourself above all people. Read it precisely. It means your flourishing gets a real vote in how your life is spent, including when someone would prefer it did not.

## Slide 5 | three | 3:00

**On screen:** Three voices compete for your resources.
**Items:** Appetite :: What I want right now. | Approval :: What I fear they will think. | Interest :: What I will be glad I built.

**Say:**

To use this properly you have to tell three voices apart.

Appetite wants something now. The scroll, the extra hour in bed, the easy win. Appetite is loud and it is not your interest.

Approval wants you to be thought well of. It says yes to the favour so nobody is disappointed. It is quieter, more respectable, and it eats more of most people's lives than appetite ever does.

Interest is the third voice. It asks what you will be glad you built in five years. It weighs consequences, relationships, the promises you have made and fair exchange.

Selfishness as a virtue means serving interest. Obeying appetite is the opposite. Obeying approval is self-abandonment wearing a polite face.

## Slide 6 | image-full | 4:00

**On screen:** Fourth unpaid revision. Portfolio: still closed.
**Visual:** desk

**Say:**

Let me make this real.

Maya is a freelance designer. She has wanted to rebuild her portfolio for a year, because it is the only thing that will get her the clients she actually wants. Thursday evening is the time she set aside for it.

On Thursday afternoon an existing client asks for another round of changes. It is the fourth round. It was never in the agreement. They are pleasant about it, and they say they need it by the morning.

## Slide 7 | three | 4:40

**On screen:** Three ways to answer the same email.
**Items:** The automatic yes :: Revision done. Portfolio closed. Resentment banked. | The honest no :: Portfolio protected. Relationship unexamined. | The renegotiated scope :: Paid round on Monday. Portfolio built on Thursday.

**Say:**

Here are three responses.

The automatic yes. She does the revision that night. The client is happy. The portfolio stays closed for the fourth week running, and she quietly resents a client who did nothing worse than ask. That is approval making the decision.

The honest no. "That's outside our agreement, so I can't do it." Her evening is protected. Fair enough. Yet she has not thought about whether this client matters to her future, and she may have spent goodwill she wanted.

The renegotiated scope. "Happy to do another round. It's outside the original brief, so it's billed at my day rate and I can have it to you Monday afternoon." She keeps the client, prices her time, and spends Thursday on the portfolio.

The third answer is the selfish one, in our sense. It serves her interest. It is also the fairest of the three, because it tells the client the truth about the cost.

## Slide 8 | hook | 5:50

**On screen:** Your ego is not your **interest**.

**Say:**

One warning before you build anything.

Your ego is not your interest. Winning an argument can damage your interests. Protecting a flattering image of yourself can stop you learning. Refusing a useful criticism can feel strong while it quietly wrecks the thing you are building.

Principle four says: hold a vision and be ruthless. The ruthlessness is about focus. You cut the habit, the excuse, the distraction or the failing method that keeps eating your resources. You are not ruthless with people. You are ruthless with your own leaks.

## Slide 9 | steps | 6:30

**On screen:** Write your doctrine.
**Items:** 01 :: What I am building and why it matters to me. | 02 :: The resource I will commit to it this week. | 03 :: What that resource is taken from. | 04 :: The responsibility I will honour or renegotiate.

**Say:**

Now we build. Pause the video when I tell you to.

You are going to write the first version of your personal doctrine. It is one page and it answers four things.

One. What am I building, and why does it matter to me? Not why it should impress anyone.

Two. Which resource will I commit to it this week? Make it specific. Five mornings, six to seven. Two hundred pounds. Saturday afternoons.

Three. What is that resource taken from? Name the real cost. If it costs nothing, go back to step two.

Four. Which existing responsibility will I honour or renegotiate to make this work? You are not abandoning anyone. You are dealing with your commitments in the open.

Pause now and write the first draft. Give it five minutes.

## Slide 10 | statement | 7:30

**On screen:** Read it back. Where did **approval** write a line?

**Say:**

Read it back. Check three things.

Where did approval write a line? Look for anything included because it sounds good to other people.

Where did appetite write a line? Look for anything that is really a reward dressed up as a priority.

Is there a real cost? If your doctrine asks nothing of you, it will change nothing for you.

Fix one line. Pause again if you need to.

- **Pauses:** slide 9 (five minutes, show a timer overlay) and slide 10 (optional, one minute). In the edit, cut to the slide with a subtle countdown.
- **Cutaways:** slides 3, 6 and 12 run full-screen images with a slow push-in (about 3% over the slide's duration).
- **Doom references:** describe traits only. Do not show Marvel artwork, film footage or quote dialogue without clearance.
- **Claims check:** no efficacy claims are made. R03 supports a philosophical argument for self-interest; it is presented as a position, not evidence.
- **Accessibility:** burn in captions; every slide's key idea is also spoken.
