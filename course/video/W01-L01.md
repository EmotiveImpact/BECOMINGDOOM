# W01-L01: Selfishness as a virtue

Video 1 of 18 • Week 1: Claim the Throne

- **Principles:** P01 Put yourself above all people; P04 Hold a vision and be ruthless
- **Model:** none new
- **Leads to:** workbook page W01-D02 (Day 2)
- **Evidence:** A one-page doctrine that commits a specific resource, names what it displaces and addresses a real responsibility
- **Protagonist:** Daniel, Black man, early 30s, short beard, freelance designer (continues into lesson 2). Second example: Amara, ward nurse.
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** SELFISHNESS AS A VIRTUE.
**Visual:** b-open

**Say:**

Welcome to Becoming Doom. This is the first lesson, and it starts with the idea the whole course stands on.

Selfishness as a virtue.

If that phrase makes you a little uncomfortable, good. Stay with it. By the end of this lesson you will know exactly what we mean by it, what we do not mean by it, and you will have done something about it.

## Slide 2 | question

**On screen:** What has it actually been allowed to cost?

**Say:**

Think of the thing you keep saying matters. The business. The book. The body. The skill. The move.

Now answer honestly. In the last month, what has it actually been allowed to cost? Which evening did it win? Which request did it beat? Which comfortable habit did it replace?

If the honest answer is nothing, then right now it is a preference. A wish with good manners. It only becomes a priority when it is allowed to take something that something else wanted.

## Slide 3 | image

**On screen:** DOOM DOES NOT WAIT TO BE CHOSEN.
**Visual:** b-throne

**Say:**

Our emblem is Doom. In the stories, he is a man who decided what he would become and then built the capability to become it. He did not wait for a vote. He did not ask whether his ambition was acceptable to the room.

We take one thing from him to start: authorship. He treats his own life as something to be built on purpose.

We also watch where he goes wrong. He confuses his judgement with a right to rule other people. We are not doing that. Your ambition gives you a claim on your own resources. It gives you no claim on anyone else's.

## Slide 4 | red

**On screen:** PUT YOURSELF ABOVE ALL PEOPLE.

**Say:**

So here is the proposition. Your life is a legitimate object of your own ambition.

Your future has a real claim on today's time, attention, money and effort. You do not need to make your ambition socially unobjectionable before you are allowed to pursue it.

Principle one in our creed says: put yourself above all people. Read it precisely. It means your flourishing gets a real vote in how your life is spent, including when someone would prefer it did not.

## Slide 5 | list

**On screen:** WHAT WE MEAN BY SELFISH.

**Say:**

That is what we mean by selfishness as a virtue. Deliberate responsibility for your own flourishing, in three parts.

You choose. You decide what you are building, and you say it out loud and write it down.

You protect. You guard the time, the money and the attention that it needs, the same way you would guard anything else you valued.

And you own it. You carry the consequences of that choice, good and bad, without handing the blame to anyone else. Notice what is missing from the list. Nothing here asks you to take from another person. It asks you to stop giving away what is yours by default.

## Slide 6 | poster

**On screen:** WHO IS SPENDING YOUR LIFE?
**Visual:** b-command

**Say:**

To use this properly you have to tell three voices apart, because all three will claim to be you.

Every hour you have, every pound and every bit of attention, is being bid on. The question is which voice wins the bid.

## Slide 7 | dossier

**On screen:** WHERE YOUR HOURS GO.
**Visual:** b-dossier

**Say:**

Appetite wants something now. The scroll, the extra hour in bed, the easy win. Appetite is loud, and it is not your interest.

Approval wants you to be thought well of. It says yes to the favour so nobody is disappointed. It is quieter and more respectable, and for most people it eats far more of their life than appetite ever does.

Interest is the third voice. It asks what you will be glad you built in five years. It weighs consequences, relationships, the promises you have made and fair exchange.

For most people the split looks something like this. Interest gets the scraps. Selfishness as a virtue means turning that bar up. Obeying appetite is the opposite of it, and obeying approval is self-abandonment with a polite face.

## Slide 8 | scene

**On screen:** FOURTH UNPAID REVISION. PORTFOLIO: STILL CLOSED.
**Visual:** b-scene

**Say:**

Let me make this real.

Daniel is a freelance designer. He has wanted to rebuild his portfolio for a year, because it is the only thing that will get him the clients he actually wants. Thursday evening is the time he set aside for it.

On Thursday afternoon an existing client asks for another round of changes. It is the fourth round. It was never in the agreement. They are pleasant about it, and they say they need it by the morning.

## Slide 9 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to answer the same email

**Say:**

Here are three responses.

The automatic yes. He does the revision that night. The client is happy. The portfolio stays closed for the fourth week running, and he quietly resents a client who did nothing worse than ask. That is approval making the decision.

The flat refusal. That's outside our agreement, so I can't do it. His evening is protected, fair enough. Yet he has not thought about whether this client matters to his future, and he may have spent goodwill he wanted.

The renegotiated scope. Happy to do another round. It's outside the original brief, so it's billed at my day rate, and you'll have it Monday afternoon. He keeps the client, prices his time, and spends Thursday on the portfolio.

The third answer is the selfish one, in our sense. It serves his interest. It is also the fairest of the three, because it tells the client the truth about the cost.

## Slide 10 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case, because this is not only for people who run their own business.

Amara is a ward nurse. She wants the senior role, and the senior role needs a course. Every week someone asks her to swap a shift, and she has always said yes, because she is good at her job and people rely on her.

So she blocks two evenings, Tuesday and Thursday, for study. She tells the rota coordinator those two are fixed. Every other swap stays open, so she is still generous. She has simply stopped being generous with the one thing her future needed.

Twelve weeks later the course is done. Her colleagues adjusted within a fortnight. One of them asked her how she did it. That is what deliberate responsibility tends to look like from the outside: a little friction at first, then respect.

## Slide 11 | poster

**On screen:** YOUR EGO IS NOT YOUR INTEREST.
**Visual:** b-panther

**Say:**

One warning before you build anything.

Your ego is not your interest. Winning an argument can damage your interests. Protecting a flattering image of yourself can stop you learning. Refusing a useful criticism can feel strong while it quietly wrecks the thing you are building.

Principle four says: hold a vision and be ruthless. The ruthlessness is about focus. You cut the habit, the excuse, the distraction or the failing method that keeps eating your resources. You are ruthless with your own leaks, and never with people.

## Slide 12 | list

**On screen:** SELFISH, BADLY.

**Say:**

There are two common ways to get this wrong.

The tantrum. You hear selfishness as a virtue and decide it means doing whatever you feel like. That is appetite with a philosophy attached, and it builds nothing.

The martyr. You give everything away, nobody ever asked you to stop, and you quietly resent every person who took what you offered. That is approval, and it is the more common mistake by far.

What we want sits between them. A written choice, a protected resource, and an honest cost. That is your doctrine, and you are about to write it.

## Slide 13 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Before you write, keep three things.

A priority has to be allowed to cost something. Serve interest, not appetite or approval. And be ruthless with your own leaks, never with people.

## Slide 14 | steps

**On screen:** WRITE YOUR DOCTRINE.

**Say:**

Now we build. You are going to write the first version of your personal doctrine. It is one page, and it answers four things.

One. What am I building, and why does it matter to me? Not why it should impress anyone.

Two. Which resource will I commit to it this week? Make it specific. Five mornings, six to seven. Two hundred pounds. Saturday afternoons.

Three. What is that resource taken from? Name the real cost. If it costs nothing, go back to step two.

Four. Which existing responsibility will I honour or renegotiate to make this work? You are not abandoning anyone. You are dealing with your commitments in the open.

Pause now and write the first draft. Give it five minutes.

## Slide 15 | poster

**On screen:** WHERE DID APPROVAL WRITE A LINE?
**Visual:** b-notebook

**Say:**

Read it back. Check three things.

Where did approval write a line? Look for anything included because it sounds good to other people.

Where did appetite write a line? Look for anything that is really a reward dressed up as a priority.

Is there a real cost? If your doctrine asks nothing of you, it will change nothing for you. Fix one line. Pause again if you need to.

## Slide 16 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission today is workbook page W01-D02.

Finish doctrine version one. Put the first protected session in your calendar today, with a start and a finish time, and write down what it is taken from.

That is your evidence. Nobody needs to approve it. Nobody needs to see your reasons. It only needs to be true.

**On screen:** YOUR AMBITION DESERVES MORE THAN BELIEF.
**Visual:** b-close

**Say:**

Tomorrow, before you do anything else, explain self-interest out loud without confusing it with impulse. No notes.

Then we take the doctrine into contact with the first person who wants your protected time. Lesson two: the courage to be disliked.

Your ambition deserves more than your belief. Give it work it can stand on.
