# W06-L03: A finite life; a chosen next chapter

Video 18 of 18 • Week 6: Become Dangerous

- **Principles:** P14 Embrace your finite nature; P01 Put yourself above all people (closing the loop)
- **Model:** All ten, chosen by relevance
- **Leads to:** workbook page W06-D05 (Day 40)
- **Evidence:** A before and after comparison, an operating code and a continuation plan
- **Protagonist:** The faceless figure: the end of the six-week arc
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

---

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** A FINITE LIFE. A CHOSEN NEXT CHAPTER.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

This is the final lesson of Becoming Doom.

Six weeks ago you were asked whose life you were building. Since then you have written a doctrine, said no, tested a label, built a system, made something real, held your ground with people and put an offer into the world. Today you decide what comes next.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** The sand keeps running. WHAT WILL YOU SPEND IT ON?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

Principle fourteen: embrace your finite nature. Your time is limited. That is not a gloomy thought. It is the reason any of this matters.

If you had unlimited time, no choice would cost anything. Because you do not, every choice is a statement of what you value.

## Slide 3 | question

**On screen:** What did you do then? What would you do now?

**Say:**

Before Day 1 you answered a scenario. Two protected hours, a favour request during them, and a project you were unsure about.

Answer it again now, without looking at your first answer. Then compare them. That comparison is some of the most honest evidence you will collect in this course.

## Slide 4 | radial

**On screen:** Six weeks. One system. Yours to run.

**Say:**

Look at what you have built. A doctrine. Boundaries. A command sheet. A Doombot. Decision rules. A feedback loop. Margins.

Your operating code is a single page that says how you will keep using these without anyone prompting you. Which tools you will reach for, when, and what you will do when you slip.

## Slide 5 | red

**On screen:** CHOOSING ONE PATH MEANS NAMING THE REST.

**Say:**

Here is the hardest part of planning the next ninety days. Choosing one path means naming the attractive alternatives you will leave undone.

If your plan includes everything, it is a wish list. A real plan says what you are not doing, and you make peace with that on purpose.

## Slide 6 | list

**On screen:** ONE PAGE. FOUR SECTIONS.

**Say:**

Your operating code fits on one page, in four sections.

My rules. The doctrine and boundaries you will keep.

My tools. Which of the ten thinking tools you reach for, and in which situations.

My rhythm. Sessions, reviews and recovery in a normal week.

My reset. What you do the day after you slip. You will slip. The code says what happens next.

## Slide 7 | scene

**On screen:** THREE BIG AMBITIONS. ONE CHOSEN MILESTONE.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Picture someone finishing the course with three exciting directions: grow the pilot into a business, write a book, and launch a second product.

They choose one sustainable milestone for the next ninety days: ten paying clients for the pilot. They write down that the book and the second product will stay undone until the first review. That sentence is what makes the plan real.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

One last case. Sophie finished the course six months ago. By month four her sessions had drifted to one a week. No crisis. Just drift.

Her sixty-day review catches it, and the reset section of her code tells her what to do.

She cuts one commitment, restores two sessions a week and books the next review. The code did not stop her drifting. It brought her back.

## Slide 9 | stats

**On screen:** Thirty. Sixty. Ninety.

**Say:**

Give your plan three dated reviews.

At thirty days, check whether the system is still running without a course to prompt it.

At sixty days, check the evidence. What does reality say about the milestone?

At ninety days, decide. Continue, change or withdraw. Then choose the next chapter deliberately, the same way you chose this one.

## Slide 10 | grunge

**On screen:** BECOME DANGEROUS

**Say:**

Become dangerous. Here is what we have meant by it all along. Capable. Deliberate. Hard to knock off course. Able to choose your response when circumstances change, without waiting for permission or a prompt.

It has never meant dangerous to other people. Your ambition gave you a claim on your own life and nothing more. That is exactly where the power is.

## Slide 11 | list

**On screen:** HOW FINISHERS STALL.

**Say:**

Three ways people stall after a course like this.

No next step. The course ends and so does the practice.

Everything at once. Three big goals and none of them finished.

No reviews. Nobody checks in at thirty, sixty or ninety days, including you. Put the dates in now.

## Slide 12 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to carry out of this course. Your time is finite. Choose one chapter at a time. And run your code without waiting for anyone to prompt you.

## Slide 13 | steps

**On screen:** WRITE YOUR CODE.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to. Take ten minutes for this one.

One. Repeat the Day 1 scenario and compare your answers.

Two. Write the tools you will keep using, and when you will use them.

Three. Choose one ninety-day milestone.

Four. Name what will stay undone, and put three review dates in your calendar. Pause now.

## Slide 14 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W06-D05, write the operating code.

Then comes Doomsday, where you demonstrate the project, defend one decision, respond to a change and take your next step in front of the Council. And on Day 42 you set your thirty, sixty and ninety-day milestones.

## Slide 15 | close

**On screen:** YOU WERE NEVER WAITING FOR PERMISSION.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

Six weeks ago you were told your life is a legitimate object of your own ambition. You have spent six weeks proving you can act on that.

You were never waiting for permission. Now you have the evidence. Go.
