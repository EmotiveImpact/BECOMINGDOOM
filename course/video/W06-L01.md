# W06-L01: Think in uncertainty

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** THINK IN UNCERTAINTY.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Welcome to the final week: become dangerous. Dangerous, in this course, means something specific. You keep your judgement when circumstances change, and you act without needing anyone to prompt you.

We begin with how to think when you do not know for sure. Which is most of the time.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** One person loved it. IS THAT A MARKET?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

One enthusiastic user can feel like proof. Somebody loved it, so surely others will.

It is evidence of interest. It is a long way from proof of a dependable market.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHAT'S PLAUSIBLE, AND WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND?

**Say:**

The ninth thinking tool is probabilistic thinking. The question: what outcomes are plausible, and what evidence would update my confidence?

You stop asking whether something is true or false and start asking how confident you should be, and why.

## Slide 4 | stats

**On screen:** Say it in ranges, not certainties.

**Say:**

Write confidence in plain words. Low, medium, high. Then say what would move it.

Low today: one keen user. Medium if three of the next ten complete the task without being prompted. High if two of them pay or commit without a discount.

The limit on this tool: numbers without a basis are false precision. Saying seventy-three percent sounds rigorous and means nothing unless you can say where it came from.

## Slide 5 | scene

**On screen:** ONE CLIENT LOVED IT. QUIT THE JOB? NOT YET.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Paul is changing career. His first client, a friend of a friend, loved his work and has told everyone. Paul is tempted to resign from his job next month.

He writes a confidence statement instead.

## Slide 6 | compare

**On screen:** Three readings of the same evidence

**Say:**

Three ways to read it.

Certain success. He resigns on one data point, with no buffer and no plan for a quiet month.

Certain failure. He decides it was luck and never tests again.

Update. Confidence low to medium. He will revisit the decision if five paying clients book by the end of March. Until then, he keeps building evidence.

Principle fourteen says embrace your finite nature. Your time is limited, which is exactly why you cannot afford to bet it all on one data point, or waste it ignoring a real signal.

## Slide 7 | question

**On screen:** What evidence would change your mind?

**Say:**

The most useful question in this lesson: what evidence would change your mind?

If the honest answer is nothing, you are holding a belief rather than making a judgement. That is fine for your values. It is dangerous for your plans.

## Slide 8 | steps

**On screen:** STATE YOUR CONFIDENCE.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Name the biggest remaining unknown in your Doom Project.

Two. State your confidence in plain words.

Three. List the evidence behind that confidence.

Four. Write what would move it up, and what would move it down. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 9 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W06-D01, name the uncertainty.

Map what you still do not know, choose one useful check and run it before Doomsday.

## Slide 10 | close

**On screen:** CERTAINTY IS RARE. JUDGEMENT IS DAILY.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

Next lesson we build the margin that keeps a bad day from becoming a disaster.

Certainty is rare. Judgement is daily. Decide with what you know, and update with what you learn.
