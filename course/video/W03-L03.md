## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** BUILD YOUR FIRST DOOMBOT.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

In the stories, Doom builds machines to carry out his will. Doombots. Copies that do the repetitive work while he does the work only he can do.

Today you build your first one. It will be far less dramatic and far more useful.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** What do you do again and again? WHAT COULD A MACHINE CARRY?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

Look at the last two weeks. What did you do more than three times? Writing similar emails. Formatting the same report. Checking the same list before every session.

That repetition is where your first Doombot lives.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** A BOUNDED AID YOU CAN REPEAT AND CHECK.

**Say:**

A Doombot is a bounded, repeatable aid that you can check. Three words do the work.

Bounded: it does one defined job. Repeatable: it works the same way next week. Checkable: you can tell whether its output is right.

It can be a template. It can be a checklist. It can be an automation or an AI prompt. The technology matters far less than the boundary and the check.

## Slide 4 | radial

**On screen:** Many forms. One job. One check.

**Say:**

Here are the common forms. A template. A checklist. A saved prompt. A spreadsheet. A small automation.

Choose the simplest form that does the job. A good checklist beats a clever automation that breaks every week.

## Slide 5 | scene

**On screen:** SAME REPORT. EVERY FRIDAY. A HELPER DRAFTS. SHE CHECKS.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Mei writes a weekly progress report for each of her clients. Each one takes about two hours, and most of the structure is the same every week.

She builds a draft-report helper: a template and a saved prompt that turns her rough notes into a first draft in her own structure.

## Slide 6 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to use the helper

**Say:**

Three ways to use it.

Trust it completely and send whatever it writes. Fast, until one wrong figure costs her a client's trust.

Never build it. Two hours per client per week, for as long as she does the work.

Draft, then verify. The helper produces a draft in ten minutes. She checks every figure against the source and edits the wording. Forty minutes instead of two hours.

And one rule she never breaks. Private client information does not go into a tool unless she knows exactly where it is stored and has permission. When in doubt, she anonymises.

## Slide 7 | stats

**On screen:** Compare it with the manual version.

**Say:**

Test it properly. Run the Doombot on one bounded task where you know the right answer. Do the same task manually. Compare the two against an explicit rubric: accuracy, time and how much editing it needed.

Then decide. Keep it, change it, or discard it. Discarding is a perfectly good result. You learned something cheaply.

## Slide 8 | steps

**On screen:** DESIGN YOUR DOOMBOT.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Name the repeated task it will carry.

Two. Choose the simplest form that could do it.

Three. Write the rubric you will use to check its output.

Four. List what it must never touch: private data, final decisions, anything you cannot verify. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 9 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W03-D05, build the Doombot.

Build it, test it against the manual version, and record whether you keep, change or discard it.

Next week we take your work into contact with other people. Week four: command the room.

Let the machine carry the repetition. Keep the judgement for yourself.
