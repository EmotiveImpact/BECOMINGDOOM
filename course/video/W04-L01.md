## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** READ INTERESTS. DON'T READ MINDS.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Welcome to week four: command the room. So far most of your work has happened alone. This week it meets other people: clients, colleagues, partners, the people who can say yes or no.

We start with a skill most people think they have and few practise. Reading what the other person actually wants.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** You think you know what they want. HOW DO YOU KNOW?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

Before a difficult conversation, most of us run a story about the other person. They only care about price. They want to catch me out. They have already decided.

Ask yourself how you know. Usually the honest answer is that you guessed.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHAT OUTCOMES MIGHT MATTER TO EACH PARTY?

**Say:**

The fifth thinking tool is incentives. The question: what outcomes might matter to each party, and how can I check?

People respond to what they are rewarded for, what they fear and what they are measured on. Understanding that makes you far more effective in a room.

## Slide 4 | list

**On screen:** THREE INCENTIVES TO LOOK FOR.

**Say:**

When you map interests, look for three things.

Reward. What gets this person praised, paid or promoted?

Fear. What would get them blamed if it went wrong?

Measure. Which number is their boss actually looking at? Most behaviour that seems irrational makes sense once you see one of these three.

## Slide 5 | stats

**On screen:** Know. Infer. Ask.

**Say:**

The interest map has three columns.

Know: what they have said or done that you can point to.

Infer: your best guess about what they want. Useful, reasonable and not yet a fact. The limit on this tool: an inferred motive is not a fact.

Ask: the question that would turn your guess into evidence.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** HE ASSUMES THEY WANT A DISCOUNT. THEY ACTUALLY WANT IT FASTER.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Marcus is a consultant going into a renewal meeting. He is sure the client will push for a discount, so he has prepared a lower price.

He runs the interest map first. Know: they renewed last year, and they complained twice about turnaround time. Infer: they want a lower price. Ask: what matters most to them this year?

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways into the meeting

**Say:**

Three ways in.

Act on the guess. He offers fifteen percent off before anyone asks. They accept happily. He has given away money they never requested.

Brace for a fight. He arrives defensive and reads every question as an attack.

Check the motive. He asks: before we talk numbers, what would make this year better than last year for you? They say speed. He proposes a faster turnaround at the same price, or a premium tier for urgent work.

Principle three, develop your killer instinct, means acting decisively when an opportunity is real. You can only see it if you ask.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Chloe wants a raise. She expects a flat no and has prepared to argue.

Instead she asks one question: what would make my role more valuable to you this year?

Her manager's real worry is cover for a team lead going on leave. They agree that Chloe takes it on, with a raise tied to it, in writing. The question found an interest the argument would have missed.

## Slide 9 | grunge

**On screen:** A GUESS IS NOT A FACT

**Say:**

Keep this on the wall. A guess is not a fact. Your instinct is valuable when it points you towards the right question. It is dangerous when it replaces the question.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** MISREADING THE ROOM.

**Say:**

Three ways people misread the room.

Projection. They assume the other person wants what they would want.

Interrogation. They fire question after question until it feels like a cross-examination. Two or three good questions is plenty.

Not listening. They ask, then wait for their turn to talk. The answer is the point.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Separate what you know from what you infer. Look for reward, fear and measure. Ask, then actually listen.

## Slide 12 | steps

**On screen:** MAP THE INTERESTS.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Choose a real conversation you will have in the next week.

Two. Write what you know about the other person's interests.

Three. Write what you are only inferring.

Four. Write three questions that would test your inferences. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 13 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W04-D01, map the interests.

Prepare a real conversation that matters to your Doom Project, and keep evidence and inference in separate columns.

The strongest person in the room asks the question. Then they listen to the answer.
