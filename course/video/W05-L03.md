# W05-L03: Make the offer

Video 15 of 18 • Week 5: Build the Empire

- **Principles:** P05 Build your empire; P03 Develop your killer instinct (revisited)
- **Model:** M06 Second-order consequences and M08 Feedback loops, applied
- **Leads to:** workbook page W05-D03 (Day 31)
- **Evidence:** An offer sheet with scope, costs and an explicit ask
- **Protagonist:** Black man, late 30s, shaved head
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

---

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** MAKE THE OFFER.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

You know your audience, their problem, your lever and your signal. Now you put something in front of them and ask for a commitment.

This is the lesson where the empire meets the market.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** "Let me know if I can help." WHAT ARE YOU ACTUALLY OFFERING?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

Let me know if I can help. Happy to chat any time. Reach out if you need anything.

These sound generous. They ask nothing and offer nothing specific, so they usually produce nothing at all.

## Slide 3 | radial

**On screen:** Clear result. Clear limits. Clear ask.

**Say:**

A real offer has edges. The result they will get. The scope: what is included and what is not. The time it takes. The cost, in money or effort. And the specific commitment you are asking for.

When an offer has edges, people can say yes to it. Or no, which is also useful.

## Slide 4 | red

**On screen:** A SMALL CLEAR OFFER BEATS A BIG VAGUE PROMISE.

**Say:**

Here is the rule for this week. A small, clear offer beats a big, vague promise.

You can always expand a pilot that works. You cannot easily shrink a promise you made to everyone.

## Slide 5 | list

**On screen:** FIVE PARTS, ONE PAGE.

**Say:**

A complete offer has five parts and fits on one page.

The result, in their words. The scope, what is in and what is out. The time, with a start and an end.

The cost, stated plainly, even if it is free. Free still costs their time.

And the ask. The exact thing you want them to do next. Book a call. Reply yes. Pay the deposit.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** I CAN HELP ANY BUSINESS. FOUR-WEEK PILOT. ONE WORKSHOP.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Kofi is a skilled joiner who has built a simple system for organising small workshops. His first instinct is to tell local businesses he can help any of them get organised.

Instead he builds an offer with edges.

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to put it into the world

**Say:**

Three ways to make the offer.

The open promise. I can help any business with anything. It sounds big and nobody replies, because nobody knows what they would be agreeing to.

Give it all away. Unlimited help, no fee, no end date. By week three he resents the client and the client has no reason to value the work.

The bounded pilot. Four weeks, one workshop, a fixed fee and a review on day twenty-eight where they decide together whether to continue.

This uses everything from week four. The pilot holds the line on price, and the review date is a planned decision point, so either side can continue or withdraw cleanly.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Leah is an illustrator. Her post said commissions open, message me. Six likes and no orders, because nobody knew what to ask for or what it cost.

Her new offer: pet portraits, A4, two-week turnaround, eighty pounds, five slots this month.

Four slots are booked in two days, and one person asks for A3, which gives her the next offer to test.

## Slide 9 | stats

**On screen:** Every response teaches you something.

**Say:**

Every response counts as evidence. A yes is a commitment to deliver. A no is information about fit, price or timing. Silence gets one polite follow-up, and then it is recorded as data too.

Your pass in this course never depends on someone saying yes. It depends on making a clear offer and learning honestly from what happens.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** OFFERS NOBODY CAN ACCEPT.

**Say:**

Three offers nobody can accept.

No price. Making people ask how much. Most will not.

No edges. Unlimited scope that turns into resentment by week three.

No ask. A lovely description that ends without telling anyone what to do next.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Small and clear beats big and vague. Give the offer edges. And always end with the ask.

## Slide 12 | steps

**On screen:** BUILD YOUR OFFER SHEET.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. The result, in one sentence your audience would use.

Two. The scope: what is included and what is not.

Three. The time and cost, stated plainly.

Four. The exact commitment you will ask for. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 13 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W05-D03, construct the offer.

Prepare your offer sheet. Tomorrow you put it in front of the people it is for.

## Slide 14 | close

**On screen:** NOBODY CAN ACCEPT AN OFFER YOU NEVER MADE.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

That completes week five. On Saturday the Council asks you to defend the value of what you built.

Then the final week: become dangerous.

Nobody can accept an offer you never made. Make it clear, and make it today.
