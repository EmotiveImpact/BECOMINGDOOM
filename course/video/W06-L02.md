# W06-L02: Preserve room to recover

Video 17 of 18 • Week 6: Become Dangerous

- **Principles:** P11 Master the art of the tactical withdrawal (revisited); P14 Embrace your finite nature
- **Model:** M10 Margin of safety
- **Leads to:** workbook page W06-D02 (Day 37)
- **Evidence:** A contingency sheet with explicit limits and trade-offs
- **Protagonist:** Southeast Asian man, early 30s
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

---

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** PRESERVE ROOM TO RECOVER.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Doomsday is close. You will stand up and demonstrate your project, and on the day something will probably not go to plan.

This lesson is about making sure that when it goes wrong, you can recover.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** The Wi-Fi drops. The link breaks. WHAT'S YOUR PLAN B?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

The Wi-Fi drops. The tool you rely on is down. The file will not open on the projector. Your best participant cannot make it.

None of this is unusual. What matters is whether you planned for it.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHAT BUFFER KEEPS A SETBACK MANAGEABLE?

**Say:**

The tenth and final thinking tool is margin of safety. The question: what buffer or fallback keeps a plausible setback manageable?

Engineers build bridges to carry more than the expected load. You build plans the same way.

## Slide 4 | list

**On screen:** HOW MUCH MARGIN IS ENOUGH?

**Say:**

How much margin is enough? Ask three questions.

Is the setback likely? Buffer what happens often.

Is it costly? Buffer what would end the project, even if it is rare.

Is the buffer affordable? Never spend more protecting against a risk than the risk itself would cost.

## Slide 5 | stats

**On screen:** Time. Resource. Fallback.

**Say:**

There are three kinds of margin.

Time. Plan to finish early enough to absorb one bad day.

Resources. Keep a local copy of anything that lives online.

Fallback. Prepare a smaller version of your demonstration that still shows what matters.

These numbers are a starting point. Set your own based on how much could go wrong and what it would cost.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** THE TOOL IS DOWN. THE LOCAL COPY IS READY.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Arif's project is a web tool. The night before Doomsday he runs his margin check. Is there a local copy that runs without the internet? Is there a smaller demo if the full one fails?

The next day the service his tool depends on goes down ten minutes before he presents.

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways the morning goes

**Say:**

Three ways the morning could go.

No margin. He spends his slot apologising and shows nothing.

Margin for everything. He spent the final week building five backup plans and never finished the main work. The limit on this tool: buffers have costs and cannot remove every risk.

Proportionate margin. He opens the local copy and shows the core of the tool in five minutes. Calm, prepared, and demonstrating judgement under changed conditions, which is exactly what Doomsday assesses.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Kieran is a self-employed plumber. Last March his van broke down and he lost a week of jobs and two regular clients.

This year he keeps a month's costs in a separate account and has a friend with a van on standby.

Same breakdown. He borrows the van, pays the garage from the buffer and loses one day. The setback was identical. The damage was not.

## Slide 9 | grunge

**On screen:** HOPE IS NOT A PLAN

**Say:**

Hope is not a plan. Prepare for the failures that are likely, accept the ones that are not, and decide in advance which is which.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** MARGIN MISTAKES.

**Say:**

Three margin mistakes.

Zero slack. A plan that only works if nothing goes wrong.

Endless preparation. So many backups that the actual work never gets done.

Spent buffer. Using the margin on ordinary days, so it is gone when the bad day comes.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Expect the bad day. Buffer what is likely or fatal. And keep the buffer for emergencies, not ordinary Tuesdays.

## Slide 12 | steps

**On screen:** WRITE YOUR CONTINGENCY.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. List the three most likely setbacks for your Doomsday demonstration.

Two. Give each one a buffer or a fallback.

Three. Write what each buffer costs you in time or effort.

Four. Cut any buffer that costs more than the risk it covers. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 13 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W06-D02, build the margin.

Complete your contingency sheet with the limits and trade-offs stated plainly.

## Slide 14 | close

**On screen:** THE PREPARED ARE HARD TO BREAK.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

Next is the final lesson of the course: what you do with the evidence you have built, and with the time you have left.

The prepared are hard to break. Leave room to recover, then go.
