## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** CLOSE THE LOOP.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

You have a leverage hypothesis. Now you need a way to find out whether it is true.

This lesson is about building a feedback loop: a signal, a time to look at it and a rule for what you will change.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** They said they loved it. DID THEY USE IT?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

You show someone your project and they say they love it. It feels wonderful.

Then ask the harder question. Did they use it? Did they finish the task it was built for? Did they come back?

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHICH SIGNAL LEADS TO WHICH ADJUSTMENT, AND WHEN?

**Say:**

The eighth thinking tool is feedback loops. The question: which signal leads to which adjustment, and when?

Three parts. A signal you can observe. A review interval, so you actually look. And an adjustment rule, so looking changes something.

## Slide 4 | stats

**On screen:** Signal. Interval. Rule.

**Say:**

Here is a loop for a simple app. The signal: how many of ten testers completed the task. The interval: review every Friday. The rule: if fewer than five complete it, revise step two, where most people get stuck.

Every number on this slide is one you would collect yourself. None of them is a prediction.

## Slide 5 | scene

**On screen:** EVERYONE SAYS IT'S GREAT. THREE PEOPLE FINISHED SET-UP.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Sam built a small habit-tracking app and gave it to ten testers. The messages are glowing. Everyone says it's great.

Then he watches two of them use it, over their shoulder, saying nothing. Both get stuck at the same screen. When he checks, only three of the ten have finished setting up.

## Slide 6 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to read the feedback

**Say:**

Three ways to read it.

Count the compliments. Ten kind messages, an unchanged app and very few users. The limit on this tool: avoid optimising a vanity measure instead of the intended result.

Track everything. Twenty metrics, no decision rule, and Sam stares at dashboards instead of fixing anything.

One signal, one rule. Set-up completion is the signal. Friday is the review. Under half means he fixes the screen where people stall. He changes one thing and checks again.

Radical accountability applies here too. You own the result your users get, not just the praise they give.

## Slide 7 | grunge

**On screen:** PRAISE IS NOT PROOF

**Say:**

Kind words are pleasant and they are not evidence. Watch what people do.

## Slide 8 | steps

**On screen:** DESIGN YOUR LOOP.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Name the result your audience should get from your project.

Two. Choose one signal that shows it actually happened.

Three. Put a review date in your calendar.

Four. Write your adjustment rule: if this, then I change that. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 9 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W05-D02, choose a useful signal.

Write your feedback plan: the measure, how you will collect it, the review date and the threshold that triggers a revision.

## Slide 10 | close

**On screen:** REALITY IS THE ONLY REVIEWER.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

Next lesson you put a clear offer in front of real people and ask them to commit.

Reality is the only reviewer that counts. Ask it often and listen every time.
