# W03-L02: Invert the failure

Video 8 of 18 • Week 3: Enter the Laboratory

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** INVERT THE FAILURE.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Most planning asks how to succeed. Today we turn it upside down and ask how this project will fail.

It sounds pessimistic. In practice it is one of the fastest ways to make something work.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** Imagine it's Doomsday and the demo is dead. WHAT KILLED IT?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

Imagine it is Doomsday, week six. You stand up to show your project and it does not work. Something has gone wrong.

What killed it? Answer quickly. Your first three answers are usually the most useful.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS FAIL, AND WHAT CAN I PREVENT?

**Say:**

The fourth thinking tool is inversion. The question: what would make this fail, and what can I prevent?

Notice the second half. Inversion is never just a list of fears. Every risk ends with an action.

## Slide 4 | list

**On screen:** FAIL IT ON PURPOSE.

**Say:**

This is called a pre-mortem, and it runs in three steps.

Set the scene. It is Doomsday and the project has failed. Write as if it has already happened. That frees you to be honest.

List the causes. Fast and specific. Technical failures, personal ones, practical ones.

Rank and act. Pick the three most likely and give each one a preventive action with a date.

## Slide 5 | list

**On screen:** HOW PROJECTS DIE.

**Say:**

Three of the most common ways a project dies.

Nobody can open it. A broken link, the wrong permissions, a file that only works on your laptop.

Nobody asked for it. It was built for an audience that turned out not to exist.

Nobody finished it. The scope kept growing faster than the hours available.

Each one is preventable, and each one is cheaper to prevent than to fix.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** ANOTHER NEW FEATURE. OR TEST IF ANYONE CAN OPEN IT.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Omar is building a small tool for his clients. He is excited and wants to add one more feature this week.

He runs inversion first. How would this fail at Doomsday? The answer comes fast. The client cannot open it. He has only ever tested it on his own laptop.

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three uses of the same hour

**Say:**

Three ways to spend the hour.

Add the feature. More to show, and exactly the same chance of showing nothing.

Write a list of fears. Twelve risks and no actions. That is anxiety with a spreadsheet. The limit on this tool: risk listing can become avoidance. Always finish with an action.

Prevent the likeliest failure. He tests the tool on his phone, a tablet and a friend's laptop. He finds two bugs that would have ended the demo. Then he adds the feature.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Ben is giving his first public talk. He runs the pre-mortem. The slides won't load. He runs fifteen minutes over. He blanks on the opening.

Prevention: slides on a memory stick and in his email. Two timed rehearsals. The first line memorised word for word.

On the day the venue laptop fails. The stick works. He finishes a minute early. Nobody in the room knows how close it came.

## Slide 9 | question

**On screen:** What one skill would make this better?

**Say:**

This week you also choose one subskill to practise with feedback. Inversion helps here too. Which weakness is most likely to hurt the project?

Choose something small enough to practise twice this week. Writing a clearer headline. Explaining your offer in thirty seconds. Testing on a second device. Principle seven, vow to become a master, starts with deliberate practice on one small thing.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** INVERSION GONE WRONG.

**Say:**

Three ways inversion goes wrong.

The doom loop. You imagine failure until you no longer want to start. Ten minutes is enough.

Vague risks. It might not work is not something you can prevent. Name the mechanism.

No dates. A preventive action with no time attached is just a worry written down.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Imagine the failure first. Every risk gets an action. And prevention is the cheapest fix you will ever make.

## Slide 12 | steps

**On screen:** RUN YOUR PRE-MORTEM.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Imagine the Doomsday demo has failed.

Two. Write the three most likely causes.

Three. Give each one a preventive action.

Four. Put the first action in your calendar this week. Pause now and give it five minutes.

**Visual:** close

Know how it dies. Then don't let it. Prevention is the cheapest fix you will ever make.
