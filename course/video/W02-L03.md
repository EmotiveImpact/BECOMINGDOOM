# W02-L03: Body, standards and recovery

Video 6 of 18 • Week 2: Build the Fortress

- **Principles:** P12 Treat your body as the ultimate weapon; P09 Radical accountability is absolute power (revisited)
- **Model:** none new
- **Leads to:** workbook page W02-D03 (Day 10)
- **Evidence:** A readiness baseline and a realistic daily command sheet
- **Protagonist:** Latina woman, early 30s, athletic
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

---

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** BODY, STANDARDS AND RECOVERY.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Every session you run, every conversation you hold and every decision you make runs on one piece of equipment. Your body.

This lesson is about keeping that equipment ready, and about what you do on the day it lets you down.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** You missed a session. NOW WHAT DO YOU DO?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

At some point in the next five weeks you will miss a session. You will be ill, or the train will be late, or you will simply not show up.

What you do in the next twenty-four hours matters more than the miss itself.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** TREAT YOUR BODY AS THE ULTIMATE WEAPON.

**Say:**

Principle twelve: treat your body as the ultimate weapon.

A weapon is maintained, not punished. You clean it, you store it properly and you know its limits. Sleep, movement, food and recovery are the maintenance. None of it is a reward you earn after the work. It is what makes the work possible.

## Slide 4 | list

**On screen:** THE FOUR FLOORS.

**Say:**

Think of four floors, the minimum you hold even in a bad week.

Sleep. A fixed boundary, protected like a meeting.

Movement. A daily minimum small enough to do on your worst day.

Fuel. Real meals before long sessions, not coffee instead of them.

Recovery. One genuinely off evening each week, planned in advance. These floors are not about fitness goals. They keep the machine running for six weeks.

## Slide 5 | stats

**On screen:** Set the floor, not the fantasy.

**Say:**

Set a floor, not a fantasy. A floor is the standard you can keep on a bad week.

For movement, something you can actually do, adapted to your body and any health constraints. For sleep, protect it, because a late-night catch-up borrows from tomorrow at a terrible rate. And one reset procedure, written down before you need it.

Physical training is personal. If you have a health condition, follow the advice of a qualified professional. This course is not medical advice.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** SHE MISSED SUNDAY. MONDAY DECIDES THE WEEK.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Lucia planned a project session on Sunday afternoon. Family plans ran long and she missed it entirely.

On Monday morning she has a choice about what kind of week this becomes.

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three responses to the same miss

**Say:**

Three responses.

The spiral. The week is ruined, so she will start again next Monday. Most programmes die exactly here.

The punishment. She stays up until two to catch up. It feels like accountability. On Tuesday she is exhausted and loses that day too.

The reset. Notice, record, repair, resume. She writes one line about what happened, separates what she controlled from what she did not, and runs a forty-minute session tonight on the first task. The plan stays.

That is radical accountability without self-punishment. You own the miss and you own the repair.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Andre has two young children. The baby was up all night and he slept for three hours. His early session is not happening.

He runs the reset. Notice: missed. Record: one line in his log. Repair: twenty minutes at lunch on the easiest task. Resume: tomorrow as planned.

It is the smallest possible session. What survives is the promise, and that matters more than the minutes.

## Slide 9 | grunge

**On screen:** NO ZERO DAYS NO MARTYRS

**Say:**

Keep two rules in tension. No zero days: something small always counts. And no martyrs: nobody earns anything here by wrecking their sleep or their health.

A missed session is information. Use it.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** STANDARDS THAT BREAK YOU.

**Say:**

Three standards that break people.

All or nothing. A plan that only works on perfect days fails on the first ordinary one.

Borrowed sleep. Late nights paid for with tomorrow's focus, at a bad rate of interest.

Someone else's plan. A routine built for a different body, job or family. Choose yours. Adapt it to you.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Maintain your body, don't punish it. A miss is information, not a verdict. Notice, record, repair, resume.

## Slide 12 | steps

**On screen:** WRITE YOUR RESET.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Your movement floor for a bad week.

Two. Your sleep boundary, with an actual time.

Three. The shortest session that still counts as keeping the promise.

Four. Your reset in four lines: notice, record, repair, resume. Pause now and write them.

## Slide 13 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W02-D03, forge readiness.

Choose one movement or recovery practice that suits your body and circumstances, protect one focused session today, and complete your daily command sheet.

## Slide 14 | close

**On screen:** DISCIPLINE IS NOT PUNISHMENT.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

That completes the fortress lessons. On Saturday the Council stress-tests your system, and on Sunday you review the week.

Then week three: the laboratory, where we find out what you can actually do.

Discipline is not punishment. It is maintenance. Do it daily.
