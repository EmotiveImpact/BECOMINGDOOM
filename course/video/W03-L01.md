# W03-L01: Think from zero

Video 7 of 18 • Week 3: Enter the Laboratory

- **Principles:** P07 Vow to become a master
- **Model:** M03 First principles (with M01 Opportunity cost revisited)
- **Leads to:** workbook page W03-D01 (Day 15)
- **Evidence:** A requirements sheet with one checked assumption
- **Protagonist:** White man, mid 30s, stubble
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** THINK FROM ZERO.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Welcome to week three: enter the laboratory. Two weeks in, you have a doctrine, boundaries and a system that survives disruption. Now the question changes. What can you actually do?

We start by stripping your project back to what it truly requires.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** Before you build anything else... WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY NEED?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

Most projects stall under the weight of things they do not need. The custom website. The perfect logo. The course that must cover everything.

Before you build anything else, ask what the project actually needs to do its job.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** REQUIRED. OBSERVED. OR MERELY ASSUMED?

**Say:**

The third thinking tool is first principles. The question: what is required, what is observed, and what is merely assumed?

Required means the project fails without it. Observed means you have seen evidence for it. Assumed means you believe it, and you have not checked.

## Slide 4 | radial

**On screen:** Many wants. Few needs. One test.

**Say:**

Put your project in the middle and everything you think it needs around it.

Now sort them. For most people, two or three items are genuinely required. A few have some evidence behind them. The rest are assumptions, often borrowed from what other people seem to have.

## Slide 5 | scene

**On screen:** A CUSTOM SITE FROM SCRATCH. CLIENTS NEED THREE GOOD EXAMPLES.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Tom wants to win better design clients. He has spent three weekends building a custom website from scratch and it is still not live.

He runs first principles. What does a prospective client actually need in order to decide? Evidence they can assess. Three strong examples of relevant work, a clear description of what he offers, and a way to contact him.

## Slide 6 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways forward

**Say:**

Three ways forward.

Keep building. A fourth weekend on the site. No client has seen anything yet.

Copy a rival. He borrows someone else's structure without knowing which parts are doing the work.

Strip it back. A simple page with three examples and a contact route, live by Friday. Then he tests the most important assumption: that these three examples are the right ones. He asks two potential clients which one would make them call.

The limit on this tool: do not throw away accumulated expertise without a reason. Some conventions exist because they work. First principles asks you to know why.

## Slide 7 | stats

**On screen:** What the extra feature really costs.

**Say:**

Bring back last week's tool. Every feature you add has an opportunity cost. Three weekends on the site meant zero conversations with clients.

Principle seven, vow to become a master, does not mean polishing in private. Mastery is built in contact with the people who will use the work.

## Slide 8 | steps

**On screen:** BUILD YOUR REQUIREMENTS SHEET.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. List everything you planned to build or do for your Doom Project.

Two. Mark each item required, observed or assumed.

Three. Circle the assumption that matters most. The one that would change your plan if it were wrong.

Four. Write the quickest honest test for it. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 9 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W03-D01, reduce to requirements.

Complete the requirements sheet and test the most important assumption. Record what you found, even if it surprised you.

## Slide 10 | close

**On screen:** BUILD WHAT IT NEEDS. TEST WHAT YOU ASSUME.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

Next lesson we look at the project from the other end: how it could fail, and what you can prevent.

Build what it needs. Test what you assume. Everything else can wait.
