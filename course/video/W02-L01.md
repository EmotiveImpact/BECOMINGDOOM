# W02-L01: Every yes spends your life

Video 4 of 18 • Week 2: Build the Fortress

- **Principles:** P09 Radical accountability is absolute power
- **Model:** M01 Opportunity cost
- **Leads to:** workbook page W02-D01 (Day 8)
- **Evidence:** An opportunity-cost ledger and one calendar adjustment
- **Protagonist:** South Asian man, mid 30s
- **Recording format:** presenter to camera with full-screen slide cutaways. Each block is one slide. **Say** is the teleprompter script and sits in the slide's speaker notes.

---

## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** EVERY YES SPENDS YOUR LIFE.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Welcome to week two: build the fortress. Last week you claimed your life as worth investing in. This week we find out whether you can trust yourself to execute.

It starts with one uncomfortable truth. Every yes spends your life.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** It only took two hours. TWO HOURS OF WHAT?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

We describe favours by their size. It's only two hours. It's just a quick call. It won't take long.

The size is the wrong question. The right question is what those two hours would otherwise have been. Two hours of your project. Two hours of sleep. Two hours with your children. The price of a yes is whatever it replaces.

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHAT IS THE BEST REALISTIC ALTERNATIVE I GIVE UP?

**Say:**

This is the first of the ten thinking tools in this course. Opportunity cost.

The question is: what is the best realistic alternative I give up?

Two words matter. Best, because you compare against the most valuable thing you could have done, not the least. Realistic, because you compare against what you would actually have done, not a fantasy of perfect productivity.

## Slide 4 | stats

**On screen:** Where the protected hours actually went.

**Say:**

Put it in numbers. The course asks for about six hours and ten minutes a week. One two-hour favour takes a third of it.

That does not make the favour wrong. It makes the price visible. Once you can see the price, you can decide whether you want to pay it.

## Slide 5 | scene

**On screen:** HELP A COLLEAGUE WITH A DECK. TONIGHT'S SESSION OR TONIGHT'S SLEEP.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Ravi has a protected session on Thursday evening. On Thursday afternoon a colleague asks for two hours of help on a presentation for Friday.

Ravi is already tired. If he says yes, the two hours come from one of two places. His project session, or his sleep. There is no third pot of time.

## Slide 6 | compare

**On screen:** Three ways to pay for one yes

**Say:**

He has three options.

Pay with sleep. He does everything and stays up late. It feels heroic and it costs him Friday, which costs him the weekend.

Pay with the project. He helps for two hours and the project slips another week. If he does this every week, the project never happens.

Name the cost first. He offers one hour at four o'clock, before his session, and keeps the evening. Or he decides the colleague matters enough to give the full two hours and moves his session to Sunday morning, deliberately.

Either version of the third option is fine. What matters is that he chose which cost to bear. Principle nine, radical accountability, means you own the price as well as the decision.

## Slide 7 | grunge

**On screen:** FREE TIME IS NEVER FREE

**Say:**

One limit on this tool. Do not price every free hour as if it could earn unlimited money. Rest is a legitimate use of time. Opportunity cost is about honest comparison, never about guilt for resting.

## Slide 8 | steps

**On screen:** BUILD YOUR LEDGER.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. List three commitments you made in the last week.

Two. Beside each one, write what it actually replaced. Be specific.

Three. Mark the one whose price you would not pay again.

Four. Draft one sentence to renegotiate it. Pause now and give it five minutes.

## Slide 9 | mission

**On screen:** Today's mission

**Say:**

Your mission is workbook page W02-D01, price the yes.

Use opportunity cost on three commitments, renegotiate one appropriate demand, and change your calendar to match.

Choose something proportionate. Some commitments should stay exactly as they are. The point is that you now know what each one costs.

## Slide 10 | close

**On screen:** YOUR TIME IS ALREADY SPOKEN FOR.
**Visual:** close

**Say:**

Next lesson we stop relying on willpower and start building conditions that make the work likely.

Your time is already spoken for. The only question is who it is spoken for, and whether you were the one who decided.
