## Slide 1 | title

**On screen:** HOLD THE LINE.
**Visual:** hood

**Say:**

Last lesson you learned to ask what the other person wants. Today you learn to look past the answer you are about to give, to what follows from it.

Most bad deals feel good on the day they are made.

## Slide 2 | hook

**On screen:** The deal is on the table. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT MONTH?
**Visual:** concept

**Say:**

The immediate result is easy to see. They said yes. The sale closed. The argument ended.

The question that protects you is: and then what? What happens next month, and the month after that?

## Slide 3 | red

**On screen:** WHAT FOLLOWS THE IMMEDIATE RESULT?

**Say:**

The sixth thinking tool is second-order consequences. The question: what follows the immediate result?

First-order thinking asks whether this works today. Second-order thinking asks what it sets in motion.

## Slide 4 | list

**On screen:** ASK AND THEN WHAT. TWICE.

**Say:**

Ask and then what, and then ask it again.

First order. The result today. They say yes.

Second order. What that result changes. They now expect the same terms next time.

Third order. What that change sets up. Others hear about it and ask for the same. You rarely need to go further than three steps.

## Slide 5 | radial

**On screen:** One yes. Many ripples. Name them first.

**Say:**

Put the decision in the middle and trace the ripples.

A discount wins today's sale. It also sets a new price anchor. The client's referral expects the same deal. Your margin for doing good work shrinks. Next year's renewal starts from a lower base.

The limit on this tool: speculation is not prediction. Each ripple is a guess. Say how confident you are, then decide.

## Slide 6 | scene

**On screen:** 30% OFF WINS THE SALE. IT ALSO SETS NEXT YEAR'S PRICE.
**Visual:** scene

**Say:**

Diego is close to landing his biggest client yet. In the final meeting they ask for thirty percent off.

He wants this badly. The first-order answer is obvious: say yes and win the sale. He takes a breath and asks what follows.

## Slide 7 | compare

**On screen:** Three answers to the same ask

**Say:**

Three answers.

Yes. He wins the sale and anchors his price low for years. Every future conversation with this client starts thirty percent down.

Never. He protects his price and the client walks, because he offered nothing else.

Trade. He holds the price and offers alternatives. I can't move the price for this scope. I can reduce the scope to fit your budget, or we can start with a paid pilot.

Holding the line does not mean refusing to move. It means you only move something in exchange for something. A clear request, alternatives and limits, all prepared before you walk in.

## Slide 8 | story

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

A second case. Ella rents out a flat. Her tenant asks to pay late this month.

First order: say yes. It is easy and kind and the conversation ends.

Second order: with no date agreed, late quietly becomes normal, and her own mortgage is due on the first. So she says yes, by the fifteenth, confirmed in writing. Still kind, and it protects both of them.

## Slide 9 | grunge

**On screen:** CHEAP TODAY COSTLY LATER

**Say:**

Some of the most expensive decisions you will make feel cheap on the day. Price the future cost before you agree.

## Slide 10 | list

**On screen:** WHERE FORESIGHT FAILS.

**Say:**

Three ways foresight fails.

Paralysis. Chasing consequences for ever and never deciding.

Only downside. Second-order effects can be good too. A fair price now can win referrals later.

No limit set. Walking in without a line you have already decided not to cross.

## Slide 11 | recap

**On screen:** Becoming Doom

**Say:**

Three things to keep. Ask and then what. Price the future cost. Hold the line, and offer options instead of a flat no.

## Slide 12 | steps

**On screen:** PREPARE YOUR LINE.

**Say:**

Pause the video when I tell you to.

One. Write the clear request you will make in your next important conversation.

Two. For accepting, negotiating and declining, write what each leads to. Include at least one future cost.

Three. Prepare two alternatives you could offer.

Four. Set the limit you will not cross, and write it down now, while you are calm. Pause and give it five minutes.
