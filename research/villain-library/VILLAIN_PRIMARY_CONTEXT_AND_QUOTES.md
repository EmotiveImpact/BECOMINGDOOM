## VP02 — Creator on Doom’s failed transformation

- Interview: A not so wonderful life: Christopher Cantwell reflects on ‘Doctor Doom’
- Interviewer: Chris Hassan; respondent: Christopher Cantwell, the comics writer
- Date: 29 December 2020
- URL: https://aiptcomics.com/2020/12/29/christopher-cantwell-dr-doom-interview/
- Inspected: full public interview text
- Scope: the 2019–2020 Doctor Doom series, especially issue #10; not a universal statement across every continuity
- Supported context: Cantwell describes Doom’s wish to change as sincere but undermined by his attempt to dictate the process and avoid confronting his own problems. The ending rejects a better alternative because accepting it threatens his existing identity. Cantwell explicitly distinguishes the character’s final rationalization from the author’s judgment.
- Brief verified excerpt, Cantwell speaking about Doom’s final self-description: “That’s not my statement. That’s Doom’s statement.”
- Locator: answer to the question about whether Doom has no good in him, near the end of the interview. This is an interview quote, NOT dialogue spoken by Doom.
- Teaching inference: test declarations through behavior and willingness to accept correction. Do not confuse theatrical certainty with self-knowledge.
- Rights: link and short excerpt only. No interview transcript redistributed.

## VP04 — Rescue, cooperation and the limits of control

- Primary publisher: Marvel
- URLs: https://www.marvel.com/comics/guides/201/doctor_strange_the_sorcerer_supreme and https://www.marvel.com/comics/guides/2184/the-dark-side-of-doctor-strange
- Works: Doctor Strange & Doctor Doom: Triumph and Torment (1989); Secret Wars (2015)
- Inspected: publisher summaries, not full graphic novel/event
- Supported context: Strange and Doom enter Mephisto’s realm to rescue Doom’s mother. In Secret Wars, Strange cooperates with Doom in preserving reality, becomes his enforcer and later defies his rule.
- Teaching inference: accepting help can be compatible with agency; a collaborator must retain the right to disagree. A successful rescue does not settle whether later rule is legitimate.
- Rights: link only; no comic text reproduced.

## VP09 — Ryan North on fictional certainty and event storytelling

- Primary interview: Ryan North Interview – Fantastic Four
- Publisher: Comic Book Yeti / Cryptid Creator Corner; host Jimmy Gaspero
- Date: 25 March 2025
- URL: https://cryptidcreatorcorner.com/episode/ryan-north-interview-fantastic-four
- Inspected: publisher-hosted interview text, especially its segments labeled 00:20:30 and 00:30:25–00:34:01; audio/video not played.
- Evidence: North explains that a writer has far more time to devise a genius’s answer than the character appears to have. He presents Doom’s flawless oratory as an unusually heightened fictional voice. The later conversation contrasts grand public promises with the takeover premise.
- Limits: the public text contains obvious transcription mistakes and lacks reliable speaker labels in places. Its time labels are navigation aids reported by the publisher, not independently verified playback timestamps. Do not quote its wording as exact audio. This is a DIFFERENT interview from V04.
- Teaching inference: preparation is transferable; flawless spontaneous certainty is a literary effect. A promise of benefit still needs consent and scrutiny.
- Rights: no transcript copied; link and original short analysis only.

## VP10 — Wisecrack writer’s argument and acknowledged uncertainty

- Primary creator article: The Moral Philosophy of Thanos in Avengers: Infinity War
- Author: Matthew J. Theriault; 1 May 2018
- URL: https://hubcityreview.com/2018/05/01/the-moral-philosophy-of-thanos-in-avengers-infinity-war/
- Inspected: full public article; sections on Thanos’s motive, his plan, and Doctor Strange’s motivation.
- Evidence: Theriault identifies himself as the writer of a separate Wisecrack QuickTake. He challenges extrapolating Titan’s scarcity to other societies and argues that greater resources offer an alternative to killing. He acknowledges a consequentialist reading of Strange’s decision while preferring another interpretation at that time.
- Limits: written before Endgame; its forecast is historical interpretation, not later-confirmed fact. Its claims about the Stones’ capabilities are the critic’s argument and require separate continuity checking. It is not V09’s script: V09’s credited writer is Joshua Corin. It is not a NerdSync source.
- Teaching inference: distinguish premise, available alternatives, constraints and predictions. Record rival explanations instead of presenting one attractive interpretation as certainty.
- Rights: no article or screenplay reproduced; bounded paraphrase only.
