## Ranked shortlist

### 1. BDS-20261025-01: group delivery does not earn an automatic presumption of superiority

**Source:** Marlene Kritz and colleagues. *Individual versus group-based interventions: a systematic review and meta-analysis of physical activity, functional, psychosocial and health outcomes*. Nature Human Behaviour. Peer-reviewed; published **15 April 2026**.
[Original article / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02429-0).

**Evidence:** 71 adult studies: 57 experimental, seven quasi-experimental, seven observational. Physical-activity comparison: n = 22,042; g = 0.086, 95% CI −0.061–0.233; I² = 90.7%. Measures mixed devices, self-report and adherence proxies. Over half the studies targeted ages 50+. Interventions lasted 4–112 weeks, mostly 8–16; these are not post-support maintenance durations. Search ended March 2024. Authors rated randomised physical-activity evidence moderate certainty. Confounding, incomplete blinding/randomisation reporting and heterogeneity constrain inference. Multiple studies broaden the evidence base but do not replicate a Doom intervention. An outlier-excluded equivalence analysis used ±0.19 bounds; this is not proof of universal equivalence. Study-specific post-withdrawal follow-up was not extracted here.

**Our appraisal:** Moderate confidence in withholding a blanket group-superiority claim; low confidence in transfer to project execution. No established lasting Doom benefit follows.

**Practical implication, our inference:** Separate three questions: does support help; which delivery format helps whom; and does the benefit justify participant and facilitator time? Day one's CHAMPS appraisal concerned a particular continuing-care package. Today's evidence asks a different comparative question. Neither cancels the other or establishes that a Council is unnecessary.

**Original doctrine candidate:** “The Council earns its place through what happens after it.”

**Original tool: Council Contribution Ledger.** After a meeting record:
- The concrete obstacle brought to the session.
- The decision or action changed by the discussion.
- The output promised and its deadline.
- The evidence produced afterwards.
- Participant and facilitator minutes, including preparation.
- What the participant believes they would otherwise have done.

The last field is a hypothesis about the alternative, not causal evidence. Attendance, enthusiasm and belonging may be useful outcomes, but the ledger must not silently substitute them for execution.

**Diagnostic opportunity, unvalidated:** Classify meetings descriptively as producing clarification, a feasible action, a useful revision, or no identifiable contribution yet. Several categories can apply. Do not turn these categories into a psychological score or label a quiet participant as uncommitted.

## Concrete pilot: compare continuation routes

This is an original feasibility design, not an intervention validated by the review. No participants are enrolled and no results exist. It refines the existing Continuation Council proposal rather than introducing another compulsory course activity.

**Participants and timing.** Use the charter's planning assumption of 6–8 adult volunteers after the six-week course. Collect two weeks of pre-pilot output records. Run four continuation weeks, then observe four weeks without scheduled pilot sessions. Record any other support that continues.

**Allocation.** Randomly allocate three or four participants to a weekly 20-minute Council and the remainder to a weekly 20-minute individual review with the same facilitator. Use the same prompts, output definitions, deadlines and reminder schedule. Each participant receives equal scheduled review time; facilitator attention per person and total delivery cost will differ. This deliberately compares practical packages, not a pure causal effect of peer presence.

**Council format.** Each person names one obstacle, receives bounded feedback and commits to one observable next output. The individual route uses the same sequence. Record deviations, cross-group sharing and additional assistance.

**Primary behavioural outcome.** For each person, count predeclared outputs completed by deadline divided by outputs due. Agree a comparable small work unit for that person's project before allocation and retain it unless circumstances require a recorded revision. Verify completion using a redacted artefact, version timestamp or private demonstration. Never require confidential work to be published.

**Secondary outcomes.** Minutes of participation and delivery; missed sessions; time to resume after a missed output; reported usefulness; continued action during the four-week observation period. Treat satisfaction and completion separately. An external review of whether the output met its predeclared criterion is preferable to a participant's impression alone.

**Missing data.** Keep missing evidence distinct from known noncompletion. Report all enrolled participants and both observed completion and a conservative rate treating missing due outputs as incomplete. Record withdrawals and reasons when volunteered.

**Analysis.** Show individual trajectories and counts, baseline differences, costs and contamination. With only one Council group, shared group effects and tiny numbers prevent a reliable format comparison. Do not use a non-significant result to declare equivalence or estimate a marketable success percentage. The pilot tests feasibility and measurement.

**Provisional development criteria.** Proceed to a larger study only if at least 75% of expected output records are interpretable, both routes can be delivered within their planned sessions, and participants can distinguish useful feedback from social pressure. These are design thresholds, not evidence-derived efficacy cut-offs. Redesign if meeting preparation displaces execution or the alternative route is materially under-supported.

**Next experiment if feasible.** Recruit multiple independent groups and an adequately sized individual arm. Pre-register the primary behavioural outcome, clustering, missing-data treatment, minimum worthwhile effect and cost comparison. Power the study for that target, not a noisy pilot estimate. Longer post-support follow-up is needed before a claim about lasting change.
