### 1. BDS-20261029-01 — test maintenance without selecting only early successes

**Source:** Carvalho and colleagues, *Are intervention effects on fruit and vegetable intake maintained after 48 months of follow-up? A randomised controlled community trial*. Public Health Nutrition, published **26 October 2026**, peer-reviewed journal article.
[Original / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980026103565).

**Scientific appraisal:** Eighteen Brazilian service units were randomised; 3,414 enrolled. This analysis selected 2,232 initial responders, including 771 classified using imputed outcomes. Mostly women; self-reported intake; seven-month intervention plus usual care. At 36/48 months, adjusted time-by-group interactions were nonsignificant; interaction estimates/CIs in image tables were not extracted. Selection after randomisation, missing-at-random assumptions, changing interview modes and uncertain cluster adjustment weaken causal interpretation. Registration in 2015 followed 2013–2014 baseline collection. Follow-up length is valuable, but neither equivalence nor booster efficacy follows. Independent replication is unverified.

**Our confidence:** Low for a causal maintenance conclusion or transfer to Doom. The useful contribution is an evaluation warning, not a proven new method.

## Practical implication and original application

**Doctrine candidate:** “Keep the denominator when the enthusiasm fades.”

Refine the existing **Maintenance Dossier**, rather than create another mandatory tool. At each scheduled check, retain every enrolled participant in the accounting. Record separately:

- Agreed opportunities and verified completed outputs.
- Known noncompletion and unavailable evidence.
- Current reminders, coaching, peer support and other assistance.
- Whether the task or its completion criterion changed.
- Whether a participant declined further measurement.

The proposed **Denominator Check** asks whether a success claim includes withdrawals and absent evidence, and whether the same behaviour is being compared over time. It is an original reporting checklist, not a validated diagnostic. An initial responder subgroup can answer a descriptive question, but must be labelled and must not replace the all-enrolled analysis.

This is our design inference. The scientific paper did not test this dossier or demonstrate that a Doom booster works. It also does not establish that all initial gains necessarily disappear.

## Concrete feasibility pilot

Use the charter's planning assumption of 6–8 consenting adults. At enrolment, define one modest recurring project output and its acceptable evidence. Record baseline opportunities for seven days, then preserve that definition through the six-week course unless a reasoned revision is logged.

At course completion and 30, 60 and 90 days afterwards, collect a seven-day window of opportunities, completed outputs and support used. Accept redacted artefacts or private demonstrations. The same brief neutral request goes to everyone, including people who stopped attending. Respect withdrawal from further contact.

**Primary feasibility outcome:** assessable records divided by expected records, retaining the enrolled denominator and reporting exact counts. **Behavioural outcome:** verified outputs divided by agreed eligible opportunities, shown separately for each participant and period. Do not classify unavailable evidence as known failure; report an additional conservative bound that treats it as incomplete.

Two reviewers independently apply the completion criterion to a sample of redacted outputs. Record agreement before reconciliation, logging burden, reasons for changed goals, and ability to distinguish ongoing support from unsupported continuation.

Provisional development gate: at least 75% of expected records are interpretable and median reporting burden is at most five minutes per check. These are invented feasibility thresholds. The pilot cannot establish efficacy or permanent change.

If the measurement system works, a later adequately powered study can randomise a defined booster against the same measurement-only schedule. Specify the primary outcome, clustering, missing-data sensitivity analyses and follow-up after the booster ends. Do not infer the booster effect from this uncontrolled pilot or size a trial from its noisy improvement rate.
