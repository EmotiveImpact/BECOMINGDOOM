## Ranked shortlist

### 1. BDS-20261023-01: precise if–then plans produce a modest benefit

**Source:** Sanne Karlsen Melum and Torsten Martiny-Huenger. *A systematic review and meta-analysis on the effectiveness of if-then plans – in a strict sense – to facilitate fruit and vegetable consumption in adults*. International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. Published 15 April 2026.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-026-01915-y) | [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12966-026-01915-y)

**Evidence and appraisal:** Ten articles, 12 randomised comparisons, 2,399 adults; active controls. Self-reported intake increased by 0.29 portions/day (95% CI 0.11–0.48); SMD 0.20; I² 32%. Outcome intervals ranged from one week to 24 months, so the pooled estimate is not a uniform two-year maintenance effect. Excluding two high-risk articles preserved the finding. The review lacked prospective registration; self-report and uncertain preregistration in primary studies limit confidence. **Moderate confidence in a small domain-specific effect; low confidence in transfer to Doom project execution.** Full-text methods, results and limitations inspected. The replication base is multiple trials, not an independent validation of this course.

**Practical implication:** Upgrade existing R05 from an archived overview with a current, bounded quantitative appraisal. Build a plan the participant can execute when the cue occurs.

**Original Doom proposal: Command Trigger card.** Fields: chosen priority; perceivable cue; immediately available response; evidence produced; actual outcome; next revision. Example: “If I finish breakfast on a planned project day, I open the prepared draft and write its next paragraph before opening social media.” A plan requiring missing materials fails the feasibility check. Treat the card as a design hypothesis.

**Diagnostic opportunity:** After a missed action, identify whether the cue failed to occur, was missed, the response was impractical, or the participant deliberately chose something else. These are descriptive categories, not a psychological score. Do not assume every failure is insufficient will.

### 2. BDS-20261023-02: continuing support deserves its own design

**Source:** Tricia M. Leahey and colleagues. *Patient-Delivered Continuous Care for Weight Loss Maintenance: A Randomized Clinical Trial*. JAMA Internal Medicine. Published online 27 May 2025.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.1345) | [Journal article](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2834317)

**Evidence and appraisal:** CHAMPS randomised 287 successful initial weight-loss participants; 93.4% completed follow-up. At 18 months, estimated regain was 0.77 kg (95% CI −0.14–1.68) with mentors plus peers versus 2.37 kg (1.40–3.34) with professional standard care: a descriptive 1.60 kg gap; the overall arm comparison was P=.002. Weight was objectively assessed. This was ongoing treatment, not persistence after all support ended. Contact patterns differed, preventing isolation of peer identity as the active ingredient. A single centre, selected initial responders and a predominantly female, highly educated sample limit generalisation. **Moderate confidence in this intervention package; low confidence in transfer to nonclinical course outcomes.** Available journal methods/results/discussion inspected; replication unverified.

**Practical implication:** Budget the post-course operating model before claiming maintenance. The present curriculum already asks for a continuation check; the design opportunity is what happens between graduation and that check.

**Original Doom proposal: Continuation Council.** An optional 20-minute fortnightly meeting after week six. Each participant shows one output, names one failed commitment and chooses one correction. A peer checks whether the correction happened; an experienced alumnus helps with implementation only after that role exists. Pilot delivery can use a facilitator without pretending that alumni outcomes exist.

**Testable question:** Does this support justify its time cost through greater continuation? Record attendance, output completion and support burden. A future controlled comparison should equalise contact time if the aim is to test peer versus facilitator delivery.

### 3. BDS-20261023-03: disciplined adaptation belongs inside indomitable will

**Source:** Hugh Riddell and colleagues. *A meta-analytic review and conceptual model of the antecedents and outcomes of goal adjustment in response to striving difficulties*. Nature Human Behaviour. Online 13 November 2025; February 2026 issue.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02312-4) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12932095/)

**Evidence and appraisal:** The review synthesised 1,421 effects from 235 studies across life domains. Only about 36% were longitudinal or experimental. In that subset, flexibility was associated with goal progression (r=0.26, 95% CI 0.09–0.45; n=1,667; nine effects). Cross-sectional dependence, heterogeneous constructs and publication-bias risk led the authors to judge the accumulated evidence low to moderate. Measures and follow-up varied; this is not one behavioural endpoint or a causal estimate for teaching people to quit. **Low-to-moderate confidence in associations; low confidence in a particular adjustment intervention.** Full-text methods, results and limitations inspected. Broad synthesis supports a research direction, not a universal persistence/withdrawal rule.

**Practical implication:** This directly addresses the repository's research queue on adjustment and sunk-cost persistence. It supports examining the charter's existing distinction between a valued aim and a changeable method.

**Original Doom doctrine candidate: “Keep command of the aim; make the method answer to evidence.”** This is our proposed wording and interpretation, not the authors' doctrine.

**Original exercise: Campaign Review.** Before a project test, write the result sought, what would count as progress, the time/resource allowance and a review date. At review choose continue, change method, reduce scope, pause, or close. Justify the choice using fresh evidence and opportunity cost. If closing, specify the next use of the released resources. Assess reasoning and subsequent action, not persistence alone.

### 4. BDS-20261023-04: an early improvement can conceal a weak lasting advantage

**Source:** Robert Edgren, Dario Baretta and Jennifer Inauen. *Habit degradation strategies promote faster early reductions in unhealthy snacking habit strength in intensive longitudinal randomised controlled trial*. Communications Psychology. Published 4 March 2026.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-026-00432-9) | [Full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00432-9)

**Evidence and appraisal:** Preregistered trial: 313 adults, 13 weeks, self-reported automaticity; substitution, inhibition and reduced accessibility, with/without app rewards, plus control. Strategies accelerated first-week decline (multiplicity-adjusted P=.042), but overall magnitude showed no clear advantage. No strategy or reward superiority was demonstrated; several null comparisons were inconclusive. Only 52% of scheduled habit reports were recorded; 35% of plans mismatched the assigned strategy. **Preliminary-to-moderate confidence in early change, limited evidence for durable superiority.** Full-text methods/results/limitations inspected; independent replication not established. Between-group effect magnitude and confidence interval were not extracted. The outcome was perceived automaticity, not objectively verified snacking.

**Practical implication:** Separate starting, continuing and recovering in the evaluation. Measure what was actually used, since issuing an exercise does not establish that participants followed it.

**Original Doom tool: Drift Check.** At days 7, 21 and 42, ask what strategy was prescribed, what was actually used, what output followed and what changed in the environment. Log spontaneous adaptations instead of calling them disobedience. For project work, use dated artefacts where feasible; do not infer behavioural change solely from a feeling of greater discipline.

**Design decision:** Do not invest in badges or streak mechanics on the assumption that this study proved lasting benefit. A simple record of action and correction is a better first prototype to test.

### 5. BDS-20261023-05: the course deadline is not a habit-formation deadline

**Source:** Ben Singh and colleagues. *Time to Form a Habit: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Health Behaviour Habit Formation and Its Determinants*. Healthcare. Published 9 December 2024. **Older foundational evidence.**
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/)

**Evidence and appraisal:** Twenty studies included 2,601 participants; 11 studies had high risk of bias. Only four reported time to habit formation: reported medians 59–66 days and means 106–154 days, with substantial variability. These are study-specific estimates, not an individual deadline. Health behaviours and self-reported habit measures constrain transfer. **Low confidence in any universal timing prescription.** Indexed primary abstract and article excerpts inspected; full-text fetches were blocked/rate-limited, so this is a limited appraisal. No new pooled timing estimate was calculated. Multiple included studies do not validate a six-week personal-transformation claim.

**Practical implication:** Keep the six-week taught duration as a delivery decision. Treat continuation as an additional phase with observable evidence, rather than certifying automaticity at graduation.

**Original Doom tool: Maintenance Dossier.** Extend the existing post-course check with optional 60- and 90-day reviews. Record action frequency, useful outputs, relevant opportunities, interruptions, recovery and ongoing support. A person still using a reminder may be succeeding; automaticity is a different outcome from valuable completion.

## Priority pilot: Command Trigger, without bundling five interventions

**Purpose:** Determine whether the card is understandable, usable and associated with better initiation of a chosen project action. This is a feasibility pilot, not a test capable of validating the whole programme.

**Participants:** Use the repository's planning assumption of 6–8 adult volunteers. No participants have been recruited and no outcomes exist.

**Design:**

1. Everyone chooses one repeatable action tied to the same personal project. Define a visible minimum output and eligible opportunities in advance.
2. Collect a seven-day baseline with the same brief daily logging used throughout.
3. Randomly assign introduction of the card after day 7 or day 14, with balanced groups where possible. Collect through day 28. This staggered start offers a weak comparative check while retaining a feasible small pilot.
4. Hold other teaching and support as consistent as practical. Record contamination if participants share cards early.
5. Conduct a brief day-42 check. Record whether prompts or other support continued. Do not describe persistence while supported as independence from support.

**Primary behavioural measure:** Number of eligible opportunities followed by the agreed action within its predeclared window, divided by eligible opportunities. Report counts as well as rates. The cue log is self-report; dated draft revisions, completed forms or other agreed artefacts can corroborate outputs. Do not collect the contents of private work.

**Secondary measures:** Weekly completed outputs; minutes of logging burden; number of plan revisions; time until the next completed opportunity after a miss; participant explanation of what helped or obstructed them.

**Missing data:** Distinguish missing logs from known noncompletion. Report completion using recorded opportunities and a conservative analysis counting missing scheduled opportunities as incomplete. Never quietly remove difficult days.

**Feasibility criteria, proposed rather than evidence-derived:** At least 75% of participants produce a feasible card after one revision; at least 80% of expected daily logs are returned; median logging burden stays under two minutes/day. Report individual trajectories and denominators. An improvement in completion is an exploratory signal, not a pass/fail efficacy threshold.

**Revise/stop criteria:** Redesign if the cue rarely occurs, participants cannot distinguish intention from action, logging becomes the main task, or the action interferes with necessary commitments. A null result should trigger examination of usability and opportunity constraints, not a claim that participants lacked character.

**Analysis and limits:** Plot each participant's completion over time against card introduction. Compare changes descriptively with those not yet introduced. Tiny sample, self-selection, shared teaching, demand effects and time trends preclude a reliable causal or generalisable effect estimate. Set a larger study's sample size only after choosing its primary outcome and a meaningful effect, not from a noisy pilot estimate.
