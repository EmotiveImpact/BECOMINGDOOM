## Ranked shortlist

### 1. BDS-20261028-01 — commitment devices warrant testing; durability remains unresolved

**Source:** Chen and colleagues, *Effectiveness of digital commitment devices in improving lifestyle and cardiometabolic outcomes in adults with cardiometabolic risk factors: a systematic review and meta-analysis*. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology.
[Original / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwag534) · [PubMed date record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42928598/).

**Evidence:** 29 RCTs, 7,534 adults with cardiometabolic risks. Eleven studies (3,200 participants) yielded +908 steps/day (95% CI 366–1,450; I²=81%); removing one outlier gave +665 (463–867; I²=0%). The abstract uses that sensitivity estimate. Authors rated step evidence moderate; 15/29 trials had high risk of bias. Interventions lasted 2 weeks–18 months, follow-up reached 24 months, but pooling used immediate post-intervention outcomes. Step counts concern behaviour; eating ratings were self-reported. Multiple trials support the category, not replication of one Doom mechanism. Predominantly Western settings and bundled strategies limit transfer. Step-count subgroup tests did not establish a superior strategy. The peer-reviewed review includes a cited preprint.

**Our assessment:** Moderate support for investigating measured activity; low confidence in transfer to project execution. Longer observation alone does not establish independence from support.

### 2. BDS-20261028-02 — distinguish a second action from sustained engagement

**Source:** Toh and colleagues, *A “growth mindset of mental health” intervention increases the likelihood of initiating self-help on a mental health app: a randomized controlled trial*. npj Digital Medicine. **13 October 2026**, version of record **22 October**.
[Original / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03322-w).

**Evidence:** 272 employees randomised; 252 analysed after quality exclusions; predominantly Asian, educated adults. Against an active control, logged repeat use over three months was 38.4% versus 29.1% descriptively. At higher baseline anxiety/depression, conditional OR=2.72 (log-odds SE=0.390); no significant condition effect at mean baseline well-being. The endpoint was completing another feature, not sustained practice. Recruitment occurred in 2023; registration in July 2025 was retrospective. Multiple moderation analyses, post-randomisation exclusions and company interests warrant caution. No post-intervention mindset check or longitudinal health outcomes established mechanism or benefit. Independent replication remains unverified.

**Our assessment:** Preliminary, low confidence for Doom transfer. A return event is a useful operational outcome, not a transformation claim.

## Practical implications and original Doom applications

Everything in this section is our unvalidated design work. Neither paper tested these tools, this programme or its audience.

### Priority 1: Commitment Contract

**Doctrine candidate:** “Make the commitment observable, revisable and finite.”

Build on the existing Council Contribution Ledger rather than adding another compulsory activity. Before beginning a small project action, record:

1. The deliverable and its completion criterion.
2. Its deadline and the evidence that can be shown privately.
3. Who, if anyone, may check the result.
4. The agreed response to a missed deadline: a brief factual account and a revised next step.
5. The expiry date and conditions for revising or ending the arrangement.

The **Commitment Fit Check** asks whether the task is feasible, the evidence is available, the observer is acceptable, and the obligation can be revised when circumstances change. These are descriptive questions, not a validated diagnostic or a score of willpower.

Our initial design chooses private, voluntary accountability without financial stakes. That choice follows the charter's treatment of agency, obligations and proportionate practice. It is not a finding that this particular form outperforms other commitment devices. A participant may decline an observer without being labelled uncommitted.

This differs from the 25 October proposal: that pilot compared meeting formats; this one tests adding an explicit commitment-and-accountability arrangement to the same planning process. It also differs from yesterday's progress-emblem test. Run one component experiment at a time.

### Priority 2: Return-to-Work checkpoint

**Doctrine candidate:** “Name the next observable return.”

After the first practice, identify the next occasion to use the skill and define the second piece of evidence. The **Return-to-Work checkpoint** separates four records: first attempt, second attempt, repeated practice, and demonstrated transfer. Do not collapse them into an engagement score.

An optional original exercise asks the participant to identify one skill they can practise, one constraint that practice cannot remove, and one feasible next attempt. This is a nonclinical teaching hypothesis, not a mental-health intervention or a copied mindset protocol. Do not collect clinical symptoms to segment the course on the strength of this study.

If explored later, compare that prompt with equally brief practical task guidance. Count a second assessable artefact within seven days, then repeated outputs over six weeks. Treat the latter as a separate outcome. First establish whether participants understand the distinction between showing up and doing useful work.

## Concrete pilot for the strongest proposal

**Question:** Can an explicit, voluntary accountability contract be delivered with little burden, and can we measure its contribution beyond the same plan and feedback?

**Participants:** 6–8 consenting adult volunteers, using the charter's planning assumption. This is a proposed feasibility study; no recruitment or data collection has occurred.

**Preparation:** Collect seven days of baseline records. Each participant specifies a modest, repeatable project action, three eligible opportunities per week, a stable completion criterion and acceptable private evidence. Record genuine changes in available time. Do not silently change the denominator after a missed action.

**Allocation:** Randomly allocate participants for four weeks to:

- A written plan, neutral reminders and private completion record.
- The identical materials plus the Commitment Contract, with results sent to a designated observer and the agreed brief account after a missed deadline.

Keep reminder timing, task guidance, available help and deadlines equivalent. Observers acknowledge receipt without adding coaching; log extra assistance and time. If that cannot be maintained, describe the comparison as two practical support packages rather than attributing effects solely to commitment.

**Primary outcome:** Number of predeclared actions completed by deadline and meeting the criterion, divided by eligible opportunities. Verify with a redacted artefact, timestamp or private demonstration. Report exact counts per participant. Where feasible, a reviewer unaware of allocation judges whether the completion criterion is met.

**Secondary outcomes:** Delay to restart after a missed action; participant and observer minutes; contract revisions; withdrawals; perceived pressure; and tasks completed superficially to satisfy the observer. Record satisfaction separately from work.

**Withdrawal observation:** End the accountability contract after four weeks. Continue only the same neutral measurement record for four more weeks in both arms; log other support still used. This probes early continuation after removing this component. It does not establish permanent behaviour change, and measurement itself may affect action.

**Missing evidence:** Retain everyone allocated in the denominator. Distinguish known noncompletion from unavailable evidence. Present observed counts plus a conservative analysis treating unavailable scheduled evidence as incomplete. Invite, but do not demand, reasons for withdrawal.

**Feasibility gates, invented and provisional:** At least 75% of scheduled records are assessable; median added accountability burden is no more than five minutes per week; observers follow the agreed role; participants understand how to revise or end the contract. Show exact counts. Redesign if the arrangement rewards trivial outputs, creates unwanted pressure, or displaces useful work.

**Interpretation:** Six to eight people cannot establish efficacy or a reliable difference between arms. Use this pilot to test materials, measurement, burden and contamination. A later pre-registered comparison needs a minimum worthwhile effect, an appropriate sample-size calculation, independent cohorts and longer post-support follow-up. Do not use a noisy pilot uplift as the power calculation or a sales claim.

## Claim boundaries

- **Established project status:** the programme and these tools remain unvalidated.
- **Supported with limits:** commitment-related interventions merit a behavioural component test.
