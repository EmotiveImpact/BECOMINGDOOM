### 1. BDS-20261027-01: package benefits do not identify the useful game mechanic

**Source:** Wenxuan Lu, Shusheng Shi and Jiayu Chen. *Gameful Digital Mental Health Interventions for University Students: Systematic Review and Three-Level Meta-Analysis of Package Effects with Component and Mechanism Evidence*. Behavioral Sciences, 16(11), 1989. Published **23 October 2026**.
[Publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328x/16/11/1989) | [Publisher-listed DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16111989).

**Scientific evidence:** Twenty-four reports were retained, but only five independent studies informed the pooled symptom model. University-student packages versus inactive controls showed depression g = 0.52 (95% CI 0.32–0.72; four studies) and anxiety g = 0.51 (0.22–0.80; three). Certainty was low, principally from unblinded self-report and attrition. Active comparisons were sparse and unstable; component evidence did not establish a consistent independent symptom benefit. These are symptoms, not verified project behaviour. The main synthesis concerns immediate outcomes; separate 3/6-month comparisons do not establish durable effects of game mechanics. Total synthesis participant N and individual intervention durations were not independently verified. Independent replication of a particular mechanic remains unestablished.

**Our appraisal:** Preliminary, indirect evidence for Doom. Confidence in a clinical package is distinct from confidence in a feature added to our nonclinical programme. Do not claim either that gamification works universally or that it never helps.

## Practical implication and original design

The report's proposed application is our own, not a validated instrument or an exercise supplied by the authors.

**Doctrine candidate:** “Progress must answer to evidence.”

Doom already has daily missions, weekly review and an evidence dossier. A decorative reward should be judged by what participants subsequently complete. Opening a lesson, maintaining a screen streak and producing useful work are separate events.

**Earned Progress card:**

1. Name one deliverable and the criterion that makes it complete.
2. Specify the evidence: a redacted artefact, a timestamped version or a private demonstration.
3. Record completion only after checking that criterion.
4. If a progress symbol is being tested, attach it to that same verified event.
5. Record whether the next agreed action happened, and the burden of maintaining the display.

Do not attach rewards to an invented personality score. The related **Progress Integrity Check** asks whether the display records opening, attempting, completing or demonstrating quality. These are descriptive labels, not a validated diagnostic scale.

This is a production hypothesis for an optional workbook/prototype variant. It does not change the six-week curriculum, imply a clinical treatment, or justify a new rewards platform.

## Concrete pilot: isolate one progress feature

**Purpose:** Check feasibility and measurement before investing in game mechanics.

**Participants:** Use the existing planning assumption of 6–8 consenting adult volunteers. No participants have been recruited and no outcomes exist.

**Preparation:** Each participant defines one modest repeatable project action and its evidence criterion. Collect a seven-day baseline using a plain record. Keep the task definition stable; log necessary revisions rather than silently changing the denominator.

**Allocation:** Randomly assign participants for 14 days to either a plain completion record or the same record with a small earned progress emblem after each verified completion. Both versions retain identical wording, reminders, feedback, deadlines and access to support. No public ranking, variable reward schedule or new teaching is introduced. This tests the emblem as an addition to the package; it does not test all possible gamification.

**Primary outcome:** Verified completed actions divided by predeclared eligible opportunities. Report individual counts, not just a percentage. A click or a claimed streak does not satisfy the evidence criterion.

**Secondary outcomes:** Time spent maintaining the record; unsupported completion claims; missed opportunities; willingness to continue; distraction from project work; and participant explanation of what the emblem meant. Do not equate enjoyment with behaviour change.

**Follow-up:** Remove the emblem and retain the same neutral record for 14 further days. Report any decline or continuation descriptively alongside other support still used. This is a short withdrawal observation, not proof of lasting change.

**Missing data:** Distinguish an action known not to have happened from missing evidence. Report the enrolled denominator, observed completion and a conservative rate treating missing scheduled evidence as incomplete. Record withdrawals and reasons when volunteered.

**Feasibility criteria, original and provisional:** At least 75% of scheduled records are assessable; median daily record burden stays below two minutes; reviewers can apply the completion criterion consistently. Revise if the reward becomes the task, completion is easily misrepresented, or participants abandon useful work to optimise the display.

**Analysis limits:** With this sample, allocation and chance differences can dominate results. Describe trajectories and usability; do not estimate a marketing uplift, declare equivalence, or claim the feature caused transformation. A later pre-registered study needs an adequately powered component comparison, a smallest worthwhile behavioural effect, and longer follow-up. Do not power it from a noisy pilot effect size.
