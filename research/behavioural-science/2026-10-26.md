## Ranked shortlist

### 1. BDS-20261026-01: comparison can help, but its incremental value is uncertain

**Source:** Thole H. Hoppen and colleagues. *Meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials examining social comparison as a behaviour change technique across the behavioural sciences*. Nature Human Behaviour. Peer-reviewed; **19 May 2025**. Older evidence, newly appraised here.
[Original article / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02209-2).

**Evidence and quality:** 79 RCTs, N = 1,356,521; mixed ages, largely high-income settings. Behavioural outcomes were required; objective measures were prioritised over self-report. Against active controls, the short-term effect was g = 0.23 (95% CI 0.15–0.31; 42 trials; I² = 96%), with very-low certainty from bias, heterogeneity and possible publication bias. Later active-control evidence: g = 0.24 (0.03–0.45; six trials), low certainty. First/last assessments defined timing; later assessments averaged 6.2 months, not a uniform post-support durability test. Eight add-on trials showed no significant incremental benefit, with very-low certainty. Many interventions bundled comparison with other techniques. Multiple trials support a signal, not replication of a specific Doom design.

**Our interpretation:** Low confidence in transfer to project execution. Treat the add-on question as unresolved, not as proof of no effect. The large aggregate sample does not resolve the uncertainty about a particular course feature.

## Practical implication and original Doom application

This is our inference and design proposal, not an instrument supplied by the authors.

**Doctrine candidate: “Use comparison to identify the next correction.”**

The charter distinguishes serious preparation and demonstrated capability from superiority of human worth. Translate that distinction into a **Benchmark-to-Action card**:

1. Select one output relevant to the participant's actual project: a short offer, a decision record, a boundary response or a small finished deliverable.
2. Choose one consented, anonymised example assessed against the same explicit task requirements. State its origin and relevant constraints. If it is a facilitator-created illustration, label it as such.
3. Compare a single actionable feature: clarity of the offer, quality of evidence, completion of the task, or how an obligation was addressed.
4. Write the smallest useful revision and its deadline.
5. Submit the revised artefact and record whether it now meets the criterion.

Do not fabricate peer averages, success rates or a supposedly typical student. An exemplar can demonstrate a standard without claiming to describe the cohort. A comparison across radically different budgets, experience or available time should trigger a relevance check.

**Descriptive diagnostic, unvalidated:** Did the comparison produce a concrete correction, an irrelevant gap, an infeasible demand, or no useful action? Record the reason without summing a personality or motivation score.

**Integration:** Use the card as an optional variant of an existing capability demonstration or Council revision. Retain the current six-week architecture. Do not introduce a public league table or extra mandatory daily task on the strength of this appraisal.

## Concrete feasibility pilot

**Question:** Does adding an example help participants understand and execute a specific revision beyond receiving the same criterion-based feedback alone?

**Participants:** The charter's planning assumption of 6–8 consenting adults. No recruitment or data collection has taken place.

**Design:** Use a common small task appropriate for the cohort, such as a one-page proposal to a clearly defined audience. Everyone completes a baseline draft. Randomly allocate participants to:

- Feedback plus the explicit task criteria.
- The same feedback and criteria, plus one relevant, clearly sourced example and the Benchmark-to-Action card.

Match the time allowance, facilitator contact and revision deadline. Give both arms a revision opportunity within 48 hours. Offer the example to the other arm after the primary outcome is recorded. Log other help received and any sharing between arms.

**Primary outcome:** Revised artefact submitted by deadline and meeting its predeclared criterion. Report the two components separately as well as jointly. Two reviewers, unaware of allocation where feasible, assess shuffled drafts using the existing demonstrated / revise / not yet evidenced categories. Record agreement before reconciliation.

**Secondary outcomes:** Time spent, number of feasible corrections identified, unnecessary copying, perceived usefulness, discouragement, and withdrawal. A claim of understanding is not a substitute for the submitted revision.

**Transfer and continuation:** At day 14, give a different task using the same underlying criterion without providing a new example. At day 42, ask for one relevant real-project artefact and record support still used. These checks probe transfer and continuation; they cannot establish permanent change.

**Missing evidence:** Keep absent submissions in the enrolled denominator. Report known noncompletion separately from unavailable evidence, plus a conservative analysis treating unavailable evidence as incomplete. Record reasons if volunteered.

**Feasibility thresholds, proposed rather than evidence-derived:** At least 75% of participants can identify one actionable correction; at least 75% return an assessable revision; median added card burden stays below ten minutes. Report exact counts. Redesign if participants copy surface features without understanding, the example is mismatched to the task, or comparison discourages action.

**Interpretation:** This tiny pilot cannot reliably estimate efficacy or establish that comparison is the active ingredient. It tests whether the materials, allocation and outcome assessment work. A larger pre-registered trial should test the add-on directly, specify a minimum worthwhile improvement, account for cohort contamination and extend follow-up. Do not derive its sample size from a noisy pilot effect.
