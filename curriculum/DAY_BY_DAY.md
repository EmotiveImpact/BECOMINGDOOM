# The 42-day course map

Planning edition. Detailed lesson and mission briefs are in weeks/01.md through weeks/06.md. Daily time includes the log; project work is included.

## Week 1: Claim the Throne

| Day | Focus | Real action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The borrowed life | Record the baseline scenario before teaching. Audit yesterday’s time, attention and promises. | Baseline response plus three approval-driven choices. |
| 2 | Your interests count | Write a one-page doctrine: what matters, what it costs, responsibilities and one protected priority. | Doctrine v1 with a specific resource allocation. |
| 3 | The first no | Rehearse a proportionate refusal, then use it on an appropriate low-stakes request. | Boundary record: words, predicted reaction, actual reaction and reflection. |
| 4 | Release the label | Choose a limiting identity claim and take one small action that tests it. | Old label, test action and observed result. |
| 5 | Choose the Doom Project | Choose audience, useful deliverable, available time and a six-week success criterion. Take the first step. | One-page project charter plus first action. |
| 6 | Council: claim your mission | Present the doctrine and project; practise responding to one reasonable objection. | Feedback, one revision and next commitment. |
| 7 | The first reckoning | Review week-one evidence. Choose what to stop, keep and protect next week. | Weekly review and next week’s protected sessions. |

## Week 2: Build the Fortress

| Day | Focus | Real action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Price the yes | Use M01 to examine three commitments and renegotiate one appropriate demand. | Opportunity-cost ledger and calendar adjustment. |
| 9 | Control the gates | Use M02 to separate control, influence and external conditions. Build an interruption plan. | Control map plus one if–then plan. |
| 10 | Forge readiness | Select an appropriate movement/recovery practice and protect one focused session. | Readiness baseline and realistic daily command sheet. |
| 11 | Keep a small promise | Complete a defined project task during protected time; respond deliberately to interruption. | Work output and daily command record. |
| 12 | Repair and resume | Debrief a real disruption; if none occurred, rehearse losing a planned session. | Constraint, controllable response and feasible restart plan. |
| 13 | Council: stress the system | Explain the operating system; receive a scheduling disruption and revise it. | Revised command sheet with a defended trade-off. |
| 14 | Maintain the fortress | Review promises and actual output. Remove one source of friction. | Weekly review with one system correction. |

## Week 3: Enter the Laboratory

| Day | Focus | Real action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | Reduce to requirements | Use M03 on the Doom Project. Mark each assumption and test the most important one. | Requirements sheet with a checked assumption. |
| 16 | Design against failure | Use M04 to identify likely failure routes and one preventive action each. | Three-risk prevention sheet. |
| 17 | Practise a useful subskill | Choose a task, baseline standard and feedback source. Run two short practice attempts. | Before/after output with specific feedback. |
| 18 | Build the first version | Produce a minimal working project output during protected time. | Prototype v1 that another person can inspect. |
| 19 | Build the Doombot | Test a reusable aid on one bounded task; compare with a manual result. | Aid, test case, quality check and keep/change/discard decision. |
| 20 | Council: show the work | Demonstrate the skill and prototype. Explain one failed assumption and correction. | Demonstration feedback and revision priority. |
| 21 | Consolidate capability | Review improvement and select the next practice target. | Skill record and next-week project plan. |

## Week 4: Command the Room

| Day | Focus | Real action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | Map the interests | Use M05 to prepare a real conversation. Identify unanswered questions about the other party. | Interest map separating evidence and inference. |
| 23 | Think beyond the answer | Use M06 to compare accepting, negotiating and declining a proposal. | Consequences sheet including a future cost. |
| 24 | Make the request | Write a concise pitch and rehearse a specific ask with alternatives. | Recorded or written rehearsal and feedback. |
| 25 | Hold a real conversation | Conduct an appropriate pitch, boundary conversation or negotiation. | Privacy-conscious factual account of what was said and agreed. |
| 26 | Debrief the response | Separate rejection, actionable criticism and uncertainty. Decide whether to persist, change or withdraw. | Debrief and a proportionate follow-up action. |
| 27 | Council: hold your frame | Role-play a reasonable challenge with agreed limits. Explain your decision. | Feedback and improved response. |
| 28 | Choose your next engagement | Review conversations, promises and project progress. | Next engagement plan with continue/change/withdraw criteria. |

## Week 5: Build the Empire

| Day | Focus | Real action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 29 | Locate the value | Use M07 to choose the work that most improves the audience’s result. | Audience/problem statement and one leverage hypothesis. |
| 30 | Choose a useful signal | Use M08 to set a measure, feedback route and revision threshold. | Feedback plan with review date and limits. |
| 31 | Construct the offer | Prepare a clear release, service pilot or participation invitation. | Offer sheet with scope, costs and explicit ask. |
| 32 | Put it into the world | Expose the project to intended users and ask for a meaningful commitment. | Offer/release evidence and actual responses, including no response. |
| 33 | Revise from reality | Review what users actually did. Improve the offer or delivery and test again where feasible. | Version comparison and a justified revision. |
| 34 | Council: defend the value | Demonstrate the offer and evidence. Explain what remains uncertain. | Peer review and one delivery-system correction. |
| 35 | Prepare for Doomsday | Identify remaining gaps, dependencies and a manageable final deliverable. | Completion plan and evidence inventory. |

## Week 6: Become Dangerous

| Day | Focus | Real action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 36 | Name the uncertainty | Use M09 to map remaining unknowns and choose one useful check. | Confidence statement, evidence and update condition. |
| 37 | Build the margin | Use M10 to prepare a realistic contingency and buffer. | Contingency sheet with explicit limits and trade-offs. |
| 38 | Assemble the evidence | Finish the project and curate evidence across the four territories. | Dossier draft and functioning demonstration. |
| 39 | Rehearse under change | Run the demo with one bounded changed constraint and use your own choice of tools. | Rehearsal output and adaptation debrief. |
| 40 | Write the operating code | Repeat a comparable baseline scenario; prepare the next ninety days. | Before/after comparison, operating code and continuation plan. |
| 41 | Doomsday | Demonstrate, defend one decision, respond to a bounded change and execute a next step. | Assessed demonstration and next action. |
| 42 | The next ninety days | Integrate feedback, name unfinished work and schedule the first continuation review. | Final dossier and dated 30/60/90-day milestones. |
