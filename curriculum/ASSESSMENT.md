# Assessment and pilot evaluation

## Baseline scenario

You have two protected hours to finish a first version of work you have chosen. Someone asks for a non-urgent favour during that window and says you are letting them down. You are unsure whether the project will work and have not asked anyone for feedback. Describe what you do, what you say, what you do not know and your next action.

Record before teaching. Do not grade assertiveness by volume, accent, speed or charisma. In the final scenario, change the surface details while preserving the competing demand, uncertainty and limited resources. Do not reveal a script that learners must imitate.

## Six rubric dimensions

| Dimension | Demonstrated looks like | Needs revision looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Agency and self-interest | States a chosen priority and owns the trade-off | Outsources the decision to approval or slogans |
| Boundaries and commitments | Communicates limits and addresses existing obligations | Avoids the issue or promises incompatible things |
| Judgement | Distinguishes evidence, assumptions and uncertainty | Treats an inference as certainty |
| Execution and recovery | Produces an output and a workable correction | Supplies intention without action or learning |
| Capability and value | Demonstrates a task and tests usefulness externally | Consumes material without showing capability |
| Adaptation | Updates a method or withdraws for stated reasons | Repeats a failing response without examination |

Rate each dimension demonstrated, revise or not yet evidenced. One missed commitment does not automatically fail execution; assess recovery and pattern. Each learner receives a specific revision request and can resubmit. Do not invent a numerical psychological score or certify an indomitable personality.

## Evidence dossier

1. Personal doctrine and project scope.
2. Daily operating system and selected execution/recovery records.
3. Boundary record and consequential conversation debrief.
4. Two decision records showing models used appropriately.
5. Capability demonstration and reusable aid quality check.
6. Live project/offer evidence and response-led revision.
7. Baseline comparison, final adaptation and ninety-day plan.

Private evidence may be redacted or demonstrated privately. A written debrief is sufficient; recording other people is not required. Assess the participant's action, not whether another person agrees, buys or praises them.

## Doomsday Council: pilot format for 6–8 people

5 minutes opening. Up to 64 minutes for eight learners at eight minutes each: three-minute demonstration, two-minute decision defence, two-minute bounded scenario response and one-minute executed next step or logged commitment where immediate action is impractical. Six minutes closing and continuation. Smaller cohorts use remaining time for feedback. Rubric feedback and revisions are returned privately afterwards. Larger cohorts need additional sessions; do not compress everyone into this slot.

Scenario choices: a tool becomes unavailable; a promised collaborator withdraws; available project time halves; a proposal is rejected. Use role-play and proportionate constraints. No sleep loss, public shaming, invented personal crises or unwanted disclosures.

## Pilot evaluation

Planning assumption: 6–8 adult volunteers or transparently enrolled pilot participants. Interview before entry about goals, expectations, available time and constraints. Collect baseline and final evidence, actual time spent, completion by day, confusion, usefulness and workload. Review a sample of dossiers with two facilitators to calibrate the rubric. Obtain separate permission for any testimonial or public case study.

Pilot success criteria are provisional: most participants complete at least five weekly evidence sets and the final dossier; participants can explain and demonstrate a personally useful change; workload is broadly close to the proposed 5–7 hours; missing evidence produces usable revisions. Record numbers and exceptions, not a fabricated transformation percentage. Interview people who withdraw.

At 30 days, ask what practices continued, what stopped, what output progressed and what support was missing. Use this to revise the next cohort. A pilot without a comparison group cannot establish that the course caused every reported improvement.
