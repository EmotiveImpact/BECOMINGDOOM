# Email sequence: The First No

Seven emails sent automatically after someone signs up on the free lesson page. Paste each into Kit or MailerLite as one automation. Plain text works better than designed templates for this: it reads like a person writing, and replies arrive in Edem's inbox.

Merge tag below is written as `{first_name}`. Swap for your tool's syntax (`{{ subscriber.first_name }}` in Kit, `{$name}` in MailerLite) and set a fallback of "there".

Dates in square brackets come from `site/assets/config.js`. Update both together if they change.

The aim of the sequence is a reply. Every reply is a conversation, and conversations sell a small live cohort far better than links do.

## Email 2 · day 1

**Subject:** Did you use it?

{first_name},

Quick question: did you use your no?

Whatever happened is useful. Maybe you said it and the sky stayed up. Maybe they pushed back and you folded. Maybe you looked at the request properly and decided yes was the right answer after all. That counts too, as long as you chose it.

Reply with one line: what you predicted would happen, and what actually happened.

The gap between those two is usually the most interesting part. Most people predict a much bigger reaction than they get.

Edem

## Email 3 · day 2

**Subject:** The difference between a commitment and pressure

{first_name},

Without looking back at the lesson, can you explain the difference between an obligation and approval pressure?

Try it before you read on. Recalling it from memory is what makes it stick.

Here is how I put it.

A commitment is something you freely agreed to. You honour it, or you renegotiate it openly.

A responsibility is something real you need to address: a dependant, a debt, a duty you hold.

Approval pressure is the demand you accept because you fear what saying no might make somebody think of you.

In the moment all three feel identical. They all arrive as "I should". The work is learning to tell them apart quickly enough to answer well.

This is day three of a six-week course. Day four asks you to use the time you protected for the priority you named. A boundary with nothing behind it just leaves an empty evening.

So: what did you protect, and what did you do with it?

Edem

## Email 4 · day 3

**Subject:** "You can move that, surely?"

{first_name},

The first no is rarely where people struggle. It is the second one.

They ask. You say "I can't on Thursday, I've set that evening aside." Then comes the follow-up: "You can move that, surely?" And there you are, explaining your whole week and offering Friday when you never wanted to.

Three things help with the follow-up:

1. Repeat, do not escalate. "I'm keeping that time. Thursday won't work for me." Same answer, same tone.
2. Leave out the defence. Every extra reason you give is a new thing for them to argue with.
3. Offer an alternative only if you genuinely want to. An alternative you resent is just a delayed yes.

And notice the guilt if it shows up. Write it down. Then check whether you have actually missed a real responsibility. If you have, deal with it. If you have not, the feeling is information. It does not get a vote.

Edem

## Email 6 · day 5

**Subject:** What 42 days actually look like

{first_name},

The First No is day three of Becoming Doom. Here is the rest, briefly.

You pick one real project at the start. Everything that follows is in service of it.

**Week 1, Claim the Throne.** Whose life am I building? Self-interest, permission, the labels you have been carrying, and your first considered no.
**Week 2, Build the Fortress.** Can I trust my own execution? Attention, energy, recovery and protecting real work time.
**Week 3, Enter the Laboratory.** What can I now do? Learn a capability fast, build a prototype, make one repeatable aid.
**Week 4, Command the Room.** Can I hold my ground? Requests, negotiation, criticism, and one real consequential conversation.
**Week 5, Build the Empire.** Will anyone use this? Put an offer or release in front of real people and revise from what they do.
**Week 6, Become Dangerous.** Does it hold together? Demonstrate your project on Doomsday, with an evidence dossier and a ninety-day plan.

About six hours a week. Fifty minutes on weekdays, a live Council of Doom every Saturday, a short review on Sunday. Nothing gets left behind: the boundary from week one comes back every week under more pressure.

## After the sequence

- Anyone who replies "IN", "NEXT" or with a story gets a personal reply within a day. Tag them in your email tool.
- Send one reminder 24 hours before applications close, to people who opened email 7 but did not apply. Keep it short and factual: the closing time and the link.
- After the cohort starts, move non-buyers to a weekly email: one principle, one ordinary scene, one question. Invite them to the next cohort when its dates are real.
