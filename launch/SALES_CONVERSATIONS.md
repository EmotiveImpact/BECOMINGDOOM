# Sales conversations

The aim of every conversation is to find out whether Becoming Doom suits this person right now. Turning away people it does not suit protects the founding cohort, and the founding cohort's results sell every cohort after it.

## Reviewing an application

Read each one within two days and reply personally. Sort into three groups.

**Strong fit:** a specific project, an honest account of what gets in the way, can protect about six hours a week. Invite to a short conversation.

**Possible fit:** vague project, or unsure about time. Reply with one question to sharpen it: "If you had to pick one thing to show people on 18 December, what would it be?" or "Which evenings could you actually protect?"

**Not now:** in crisis, looking for therapy, wants permission to be cruel, or cannot give the time. Say so kindly and offer the next cohort list. Where someone seems to need professional support, say that plainly and point them to it.

## Reply to a strong application

> Hi [name], thanks for applying. I read your answers. [One specific line about their project or obstacle.] It sounds like a good fit. Could we have a quick chat before I send the link? Either a few messages here or a 15 minute call, whichever is easier for you.

## The conversation

Keep it short. Ask, listen, reflect, check fit, offer.

1. **Open:** "Did you get a chance to try the First No? What happened?"
2. **Listen:** "What is the project you keep putting off? What usually gets in the way?" Let them describe it in their own words. Do not pitch yet.
3. **Reflect:** "So the project is X, and the pattern is that time keeps going to Y." Check you have it right.
4. **Fit check:** "The course asks for about six hours a week for six weeks, a live Council every Saturday and one real project you put in front of people in week five. Is that realistic for you right now?"
5. **Offer:** "The founding cohort starts [6 November], [8] places, [£197]. Every lesson, the Saturday Council and direct feedback from me each week. Would you like the link to secure your place?"
6. **If yes:** send the checkout link with the start date, the time of the first Council and what to prepare (one sentence describing their project).
7. **If no:** accept it. "That is a good no. Want me to let you know when the next cohort opens?"

## Honest answers to common objections

**"I don't have time."** Then this might be the wrong six weeks, and that is fine. If it is close, we can reduce your project's scope at the start. Ask: "Which three evenings could you actually protect?"

**"It's a lot of money right now."** Do not argue with this. Ask if it is the money or whether they are unsure it will be worth it. If it is the money, offer the next cohort list. Do not offer instalments or discounts on the spot without deciding that policy in advance for everyone.

**"How do I know it works?"** Tell the truth: "It hasn't run in full yet, so I can't show you results. The curriculum is complete, you can see a full lesson already, and you get a refund if you do the week one work and decide it isn't for you."

**"Is this some kind of hustle bro thing?"** "No income promises. The course is about protecting a priority, thinking clearly and building something real. The free lesson is the most honest preview."

**"Selfishness sounds toxic."** "We mean responsibility for your own flourishing, keeping your real commitments and stopping giving away what you never agreed to. Cruelty earns no credit in the course."

## Follow-up

One follow-up after two days if they went quiet:

> Hi [name], just checking in on this. No pressure either way. Places close [Thursday 5 November].

Then leave it. No third message.

## Things never to say

- Any promise of income, a job, confidence or transformation.
- Any claim about what past participants achieved. There are none yet.
- "Only 2 places left" unless that is exactly true at that moment.
- Anything that pressures someone to decide on the spot.
