The pattern this course is built to break

You can describe an impressive future. Then today arrives and you spend it protecting other people's impression of you: the favour you did not want to do, the extra revision nobody paid for, the project you abandoned when it got hard or someone frowned at it.

Becoming Doom starts from a proposition most people were taught to be ashamed of: your own life deserves your investment. We call it selfishness as a virtue. That means deliberate responsibility for your own flourishing, with long-term consequences, relationships and real commitments counted in. Impulse does not qualify.

Self-interest gives the mission legitimacy. Courage to be disliked protects it. Clear thinking improves it. Capability gives it effect.

Six weeks. Each one builds on the last.

Nothing is left behind. The boundary from week one returns under more pressure in every week after it, alongside each new tool.

  1. 1

    Claim the Throne

    Whose life am I actually building?

    Self-interest, permission, limiting labels and your first considered no. You leave with a personal doctrine, a baseline and a charter for your project.

  2. 2

    Build the Fortress

    Can I trust my own execution?

    Attention, energy, recovery, commitments and opportunity cost. You test a daily command sheet and protect real work time.

  3. 3

    Enter the Laboratory

    What can I now do?

    Investigation, deliberate skill practice, a prototype and one repeatable aid or automation you have checked works.

  4. 4

    Command the Room

    Can I hold my ground?

    Requests, negotiation, criticism and tactical withdrawal. You prepare for, hold and honestly debrief one real consequential conversation.

  5. 5

    Build the Empire

    Will anyone use this?

    Value, offer, leverage and feedback. You put a live offer or release in front of real people and revise from their responses.

  6. 6

    Become Dangerous

    Does it hold together?

    Uncertainty, contingencies and independent judgement. You demonstrate your project on Doomsday, with an evidence dossier and a ninety-day plan.

What you get

  • Eighteen short lessons, three a week, each ending in a mission you carry out in your own life the same day
  • A daily workbook page for all 42 days, with a five-minute command record: priority, pressure, response, evidence, correction
  • The Council of Doom: a 75-minute live session every Saturday for demonstrations, feedback and rehearsal
  • Direct written feedback on your weekly evidence from Edem, who designed the course
  • Ten reasoning tools introduced two at a time from week two, with practice in choosing the right one
  • The Doomsday demonstration on 18 December, and a ninety-day continuation plan to take with you

The weekly rhythm

Monday to Friday, about 50 minutes a day: a quick recall, up to ten minutes of teaching, thirty minutes of action on your project and a short review. Saturday is Council. Sunday is a 45-minute review and plan. Around six hours a week in total, and the project work is the mission, so there is no second workload stacked on top.

What you will be able to show at the end

Completion rests on evidence, not attendance or streaks:

A personal doctrine

What matters to you, what it costs and what you protect, written and tested.

A working daily system

Tested under real conditions, with a record of how you recovered when it broke.

A demonstrated capability

Something useful you can now do, shown to others, plus one repeatable aid.

A project in the world

Tested with the people it is for, revised from what they actually did.

Who this is for

  • Ambitious adults whose effort keeps getting scattered by approval-seeking, overcommitment or other people's expectations
  • People with a project they keep postponing, who want something concrete to show for six weeks
  • Anyone willing to do about six hours of real work a week and bring it to a small group

Who it is not for

  • Anyone looking for permission to be cruel or to ignore commitments they freely made
  • Anyone who wants to watch videos without acting on them
  • Anyone in crisis who needs clinical or professional support. This is education, not therapy.

Questions

Is "Doom" meant to be dark?

It is meant to be serious. The name borrows from fiction's most uncompromising figure as an opening lens, then every lesson moves to an ordinary decision in your real life. "Become Dangerous" means capable, prepared and hard to knock off course.

Does selfishness mean ignoring other people?

No. We teach self-interest that counts long-term consequences, relationships, commitments and fair exchange. You learn to honour what you freely committed to and stop handing away what you did not.

What if I miss a day?

Recovery is taught explicitly: notice, record, repair, resume. A lapse handled well is evidence of progress. Nobody is assessed on a perfect streak.

What if I can only do less than six hours?

Tell us when you apply. You can reduce your project's scope with Edem at the start rather than falling behind halfway through.

Do I need a business idea?

No. The Doom Project can be a body of creative work, a skill, a career move, a community project or a business. It needs an intended audience you can test it with in week five.

Does physical training form part of it?

Energy and recovery are taught, and you choose physical activity that suits your body and circumstances. There is no universal training programme and nothing is required that is unsafe for you.

Will I make money from this?

We make no income promises. Week five has you put an offer or release in front of real people. What happens next depends on your project, your market and your work.

What is the refund policy?

If you complete the week one work and decide Becoming Doom is not for you, ask for a refund before week two begins and you will get your money back in full.
